The WORX Landroid Vision Cloud 4WD is the most approachable way to put a robot mower on a hilly lawn. It keeps the antenna-free RTK Cloud setup and easy app of the standard Vision Cloud, then adds four-wheel drive and an 84% slope rating that matches the AWD flagships. The catch is a narrow 8.7" deck on a machine sold for up to 1.5 acres, so it wins on hills and convenience, not on speed.

## Who the Vision Cloud 4WD is for (and who should skip it)

It's for owners of sloped or bumpy lawns from a quarter acre to an acre and a half who want a mainstream brand with big-box returns. It's especially appealing if an RTK mast would be awkward on your property, or if you already own WORX Power Share batteries.

Skip it if:

- **You have a big, open, mostly flat lawn.** A wider deck will finish in a fraction of the time. Look at the Navimow X4 or LUBA 3 AWD (below).
- **Your zones connect through a narrow side yard.** One hands-on test found it stalled in tight passages (details below).
- **You want the lowest price per acre.** Top tiers run up to about $3,699, which puts it against mowers with twice the deck width.

## Navigation and setup

The 4WD uses the same stack as its two-wheel sibling: network RTK, a stereo camera with V-SLAM, and AI obstacle avoidance. RTK corrections come over 4G or Wi-Fi from WORX's RTK Cloud, so nothing gets staked in the yard. When trees block the sky, the camera system takes over. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) covers why that hybrid matters.

Freshly Charged's week-long review was the most positive we found. Its testers called it the first robot mower that delivered on "no external RTK station, a base station that actually sits against your house, and edge trimming that actually reduces the need for a string trimmer." They logged "zero connection losses over the full week," and they reported that the auto-mapper started 3 to 4 inches inside the yard edge, then expanded to the concrete borders on its own. One caveat from that review: the first firmware update took about four hours on a weak connection, so start setup on a day you're not in a hurry.

SmartHomeAssistent.de, a German site that tested the 1-acre WR344E, also found automatic mapping quick and precise, with notably straight stripes.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

This is why you buy the 4WD. WORX rates it for 84% grade (about 40 degrees), with independent wheel motors on a terrain-adaptive chassis and front steering meant to avoid scuffing turf on turns. On paper, that matches the [Segway Navimow X4](/mowers/segway-navimow-x4/) and edges past the 80% of the LUBA 3 AWD. As always, manufacturer slope figures assume dry, short grass. Our [slope limits guide](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/) explains how much margin to leave.

In practice, SmartHomeAssistent.de reported it "handled slopes over 45% without difficulty," and Freshly Charged saw it cross gravel, packed dirt and a sloped driveway cleanly. The weakness came in tight spaces. The German testers found it got stuck in a passage about 60 cm (24") wide without sending an app notification, and they called it unreliable in gaps under roughly 80 cm (31"). Measure gates and side yards before you buy.

## Coverage, cutting and edges

Here's the tradeoff. An 8.7" deck on a 1.5-acre machine means long mowing hours. In the German test, a 200 m² (about 2,150 sq ft) area took roughly 170 minutes including charging breaks and used about 143% of a battery. WORX quotes a 60-minute recharge for the 20V 5Ah pack. The 4WD is built to mow a little every day, not to finish quickly.

Edges are a strength if your unit has WORX's Cut-to-Zero offset blade. Freshly Charged's tester rated edge performance nine out of ten after three days of the Smart Trim feature learning the yard. SmartHomeAssistent.de was more measured, calling edges "decent" but still leaving small strips against borders. WORX includes Cut-to-Zero on some Vision Cloud SKUs and sells it as a module for others, so confirm what's in the box. Cutting height runs 1.57" to 3.54", adjusted electronically.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

You get 4G, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, plus GPS/4G tracking and an alarm. WORX advertises lifetime free RTK Cloud service and free 4G, which is a real saving over rivals that bill for cellular after year one. Our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) covers what tracking does in practice.

One app gripe from Freshly Charged: you can pause an active job but not cancel it outright.

## Ownership notes

- **Retail and parts.** WORX is sold at Home Depot, Lowe's, Best Buy, Walmart and Amazon, which makes returns and replacement parts easier than with direct-only brands.
- **Pricing.** The 1-acre model was about $2,400 in spring 2026. The series runs about $1,999 to $3,699 across four sizes (WR341 to WR346, ¼ to 1½ acres). Prices move often.
- **Handling.** It weighs about 40 lbs with the battery. Freshly Charged noted the handle placement means lifting it from the side when it's docked.
- **Not published:** WORX doesn't list a noise figure or IP rating for this model. See our [rain guide](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/) for general weather advice.

The [Navimow X4](/mowers/segway-navimow-x4/) is the obvious rival. It has the same slope rating and antenna-free network RTK with a deck nearly twice as wide, so on big lawns it simply finishes faster. The [Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD](/mowers/mammotion-luba-3-awd/) adds LiDAR, which helps under trees. The [Sunseeker X7 Gen 2](/mowers/sunseeker-x7-gen-2/) is another wide-deck AWD option at a similar price. Where the WORX wins is retail convenience, shared tool batteries and free cellular.

## Bottom line

The Vision Cloud 4WD gives you flagship-level hill climbing without an antenna in the yard, from a brand you can return at Home Depot. It's an easy recommendation for steep lawns up to about an acre with no tight passages. On bigger, flatter properties, the narrow deck becomes the story, and a wider-cutting rival is the better buy. Expect to pay about $2,000 to $3,700 depending on size (October 2026), and check for sales.
