The ECOVACS GOAT O1000 LiDAR Pro is the cheapest robot mower that also trims your edges. It pairs dual-LiDAR navigation with a built-in TruEdge string trimmer, for a typical price of about $999 to $1,499. For a compact, flat-to-gentle yard where edging is the chore you hate most, little else at this price comes close. The big caveats are a quarter-acre ceiling, a loud trimmer, and no listed 4G or theft tracking.

## Who the GOAT O1000 is for (and who should skip it)

It's built for yards up to a quarter acre with lots of edges: paths, driveways, flower beds and lawn-to-patio borders where a normal robot leaves a fringe of long grass. LiDAR navigation also makes it a good fit for small yards with trees or a house that shades the lawn.

Skip it if:

- **Your lawn is bigger than about 10,000 sq ft.** Look at the GOAT A2000/A3000 LiDAR Pro instead (below).
- **Your yard is visible from the street and you want tracking.** ECOVACS doesn't list 4G or GPS anti-theft for this model.
- **You have hills.** At 45% (about 24°) with two-wheel drive, it's for flat to moderate grades.

## Navigation and setup

ECOVACS calls the system HoloScope 360: a spinning 360° LiDAR plus a 3D time-of-flight LiDAR, backed by an AI camera for obstacle recognition. Because it builds its map from the surroundings rather than from satellites, tree canopy and walls don't throw it off the way they can an RTK mower. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) goes deeper on LiDAR versus RTK.

There's no antenna and no wire. ECOVACS supports automatic mapping or remote-control mapping in the app, claims 0.79" positioning precision, and says the AIVI 3D system recognizes more than 200 obstacle types. On paper it squeezes through passages as narrow as 31.5", which helps in side yards.

Independent US coverage of the O1000 LiDAR Pro is still thin. Most of what's online is spec recaps and deal posts, including 9to5Toys flagging a $999 low in July 2026. We'll update this page as more major reviewers publish. In the meantime, treat obstacle-avoidance claims as marketing until owners confirm them, and see our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) for general mapping tips.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

The O1000 is a two-wheel-drive mower rated for 45% slopes (about 24°) and 1.2" obstacles. That rating, like all of them, assumes dry, short grass. It's comfortable on a gently rolling suburban lot but not on a real hillside. Use the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) if you're unsure.

ECOVACS quotes about 120 m²/hour in its Delicate mode and 180 m²/hour in Efficient mode (roughly 1,300 to 1,950 sq ft per hour). With a 90-minute runtime and a 70-minute recharge, a full quarter acre takes several cycles, which is normal for this class. If your mowable area sits near the 10,764 sq ft rating, expect the mower to run most days in peak season. Our [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) helps you check whether you have enough headroom.

## Coverage, cutting and the TruEdge trimmer

The deck is 8.66" wide with a cutting range of 1.18" to 3.15". That's a low-ish ceiling if you keep cool-season grass tall in summer.

TruEdge is the headline. A small string trimmer spinning at 8,500 rpm sits beside the deck, and ECOVACS claims it reaches "0 inch" boundary cutting. European reviews of the closely related GOAT O1200 LiDAR Pro, sold abroad with a larger area rating, found the trimmer improves edges significantly but doesn't match a hand-held trimmer. Expect fewer trimming sessions, not zero. Raised curbs, fence corners and tight inside angles will still need occasional touch-up.

The trimmer's cost is noise: about 81 dB, against 61 dB for normal mowing. Schedule edging for daytime if neighbors are close.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

The O1000 LiDAR Pro runs on the ECOVACS Home app over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. You get zones, no-go areas and remote mapping. What you don't get, per ECOVACS's own listing, is 4G or GPS theft tracking. For a mower that may sit in a front yard, that's the most important gap. Our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) covers workarounds, and ECOVACS has offered garage bundles in some markets that at least keep it out of sight.

## Ownership notes

- **Weatherproofing.** Both mower and dock are rated IPX6, so a hose-down is fine. Our [rain guide](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/) covers wet-weather mowing.
- **Consumables.** You'll replace razor blades and trimmer line. ECOVACS sells blade-kit bundles, so price those in. See our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/).
- **Battery.** The 5,200 mAh pack is listed as non-removable in third-party spec listings, so long-term battery replacement may mean a service visit. See [how long robot mowers last](/posts/how-long-do-robot-mowers-last/).
- **Price swings.** List is $1,499. It has sold for $999 in summer 2026, so watch for sales.

## Bottom line

If you have a small, gently sloped yard and edging is the part of mowing you'd most like to quit, the GOAT O1000 LiDAR Pro is the cheapest way to get a robot that tries to do it for you. Accept that the trimmer is loud and won't make your string trimmer obsolete, and plan around the lack of theft tracking.
