The Airseekers Tron is an RTK-guided robot mower with the best mulching any reviewer has reported, wrapped in a body that looks like a sci-fi transport. At about $1,299 for the standard model, down from a $1,999 list price, it's now reasonably priced for 0.59 acre. It isn't a polished machine, though. Reviewers found weak obstacle avoidance, a bare-bones app and odd front wheels that make tight turns hard, so it suits simple, open yards with thick grass, not cluttered ones.

## Who the Airseekers Tron is for (and who should skip it)

The Tron makes the most sense on an open lawn with few obstacles, gentle-to-moderate slopes and grass that grows fast. New Atlas's Joe Salas found it "absolutely knocks down the tall stuff that other mowers refuse to touch," and its mulching leaves clippings almost invisible. There are three sizes: the SE (0.37 acre), the Tron (0.59 acre) and the PLUS (1 acre).

Skip it if your yard has lots of trees, beds, walls or narrow passages. New Atlas found narrow spaces are "where the Tron struggles," and its obstacle avoidance needs plenty of no-go zones to compensate. Skip it if you want a well-designed app, too. And if you want to mow at night, only the PLUS has the lighting Airseekers says it needs.

## Navigation and setup

The Tron combines RTK positioning with VSLAM cameras: a 300° five-camera AirVision array on the Tron and PLUS, and a single camera on the SE. An RTK base station is included, and Airseekers also advertises network RTK (NRTK). That sounds flexible, but New Atlas found the two can't be combined: "RTK maps aren't compatible with NRTK maps," so you commit to one. Android Headlines found network RTK struggled with the shade in its yard and used the base. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) covers why RTK of either kind dislikes tree cover.

Setup has quirks. New Atlas praised the unusually tall RTK antenna, which cleared a privacy fence, but found the antenna and dock cables were only about 3 ft long. Android Headlines noted that no mounting arm is included and that moving the base later forces a complete remap. Despite Airseekers' auto-mapping claims, Android Headlines had to map manually, and channel setup is "not as straightforward as some of its competitors," according to Android Headlines.

The 30° toe-in front wheels make manual driving frustrating. New Atlas said it's "impossible to drive in a straight line," which matters because you drive the mower to draw boundaries, channels and no-go zones. Airseekers' own instructions suggest curved corners rather than 90° angles in your maps.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

Airseekers rates the Tron and PLUS for 65% grade (33°). The SE is rated 60% (31°) while working and 65% while driving. New Atlas didn't have a slope steep enough to test the rating and said "I'll believe it when I see it." Treat it as a best case for dry, short grass, and check your yard with the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/).

Turning is the weak point. The rear wheels steer the long chassis, and New Atlas found the front omni-wheels "get hung up on every irregularity," such as slightly recessed stepping stones. That triggered "motor abnormal" and overheat errors. The rear motors sometimes struggled to complete a U-turn on cut grass.

Obstacle avoidance is the biggest complaint. New Atlas watched the Tron give people "a little love tap" about half the time, push a branch into its camera, climb a low stone wall before it was zoned out, and hit a fountain wall repeatedly until it shut down, without notifying the app. Android Headlines was kinder: it spotted moving balls but struggled with frameless glasses, and the bumper serves as a backup. New Atlas's rule of thumb sums it up: "When in doubt, no-go it out."

## Coverage, cutting and edges

Cut quality is where the Tron earns its place. The standard 8.66" disc uses a stacked dual-blade design with a propeller above it that lifts grass into the blades. New Atlas reported not one clump in roughly eight weeks, and Android Headlines described a "carpet-like finish" with nice lines even in 3" to 4" grass. The Tron and PLUS accept an optional 11.8" disc, which New Atlas fitted in about five minutes and found finished jobs noticeably faster. One caution: the propeller is plastic, and New Atlas saw one shatter on a pinecone.

Airseekers' product page claims about 0.21 to 0.29 acre per charge and up to an acre a day, and New Atlas cites about three hours of runtime per charge. Cutting height runs from 1.18" to 3.54". Edges are only average. Android Headlines said it didn't cut as close as hoped without reordering the mowing sequence, so keep the string trimmer.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

New Atlas didn't mince words: "The app sucks." It's metric-only, shows progress only while the mower is moving, and doesn't tell you when a job finishes or stalls. Android Headlines called it "85% of the way there," noting deep mowing controls (sequence, angle, path width) but no rain settings. You can set up to 80 zones.

Anti-theft is limited to real-time GPS tracking with location alerts over 4G, with no Find My support. See our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) for extra layers worth adding.

## Ownership notes

- **Rain:** it's rated IPX6, but Airseekers told New Atlas it doesn't recommend mowing in rain. Soft, sandy or muddy soil can rut. Our [rain guide](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/) explains why.
- **Night mowing:** New Atlas advises against it on the standard Tron, which lacks a proper headlight and can drift into no-go zones in the dark.
- **Parts:** the battery is removable, spare blades are included, and Airseekers sells discs and fan blades.
- **Network RTK:** it's complimentary, but New Atlas couldn't find how long it stays free.
- **Pricing:** list prices are $1,299, $1,999 and $2,299; October 2026 prices were about $1,099, $1,299 and $2,099.

## How the Airseekers Tron compares

The [Segway Navimow X4](/mowers/segway-navimow-x4/) costs more, at about $2,499 to $2,999, but brings AWD, an 84% rating, a 17" deck and a far more polished app. The [WORX Landroid Vision Cloud](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud/) is the budget network-RTK alternative: about $850 to $1,840, easy setup, and big-box-store support, though it's rated for only 30% slopes. For an acre or more of open grass, the [Segway Navimow X3](/mowers/segway-navimow-x3/) is a better-reviewed 2WD option rated for 50% slopes.

## Bottom line

The Airseekers Tron is a likable oddball. On an open, simple lawn with thick grass, it mulches better than anything reviewers have tried, handles tall growth and runs for hours. Its navigation, obstacle avoidance and app trail the mainstream brands, and its wheels make cluttered yards hard work. At about $1,299 it's fair value for the right yard. Map generously with no-go zones and it rewards you.
