The HOOKII Neomow X2 is the cheapest way to get LiDAR robot mowing on a large lawn: the SE covers three-quarters of an acre for about $1,300, and the Pro stretches to 1.5 acres for about $2,500. It needs no RTK antenna, cuts an 11" swath and runs quietly. The catches are real, though: a two-wheel-drive chassis rated for gentle slopes only, camera features that cost $339 extra below the Pro, and very little independent testing so far.

## Who the Neomow X2 is for (and who should skip it)

The X2 makes sense for large, mostly gentle lawns where satellite mowers struggle: a big suburban lot with scattered mature trees, or a property where the house and outbuildings block much of the sky. Four tiers cover 0.37, 0.75, 1 and 1.5 acres, so there's a size for most US yards.

Skip it if your yard is steep. A 45% rating (about 24°) is fine for mild grades but not for real hills, where the AWD [Dreame A3 AWD](/mowers/dreame-a3-awd/) or [MOVA LiDAX Ultra AWD](/mowers/mova-lidax-ultra-awd/) belong. Skip it if you want a mower with a long trail of independent reviews. And if your lawn is busy with toys and pets, factor in the vision module or step up to the Pro, which changes the value math.

## Navigation and setup

All X2 models use a 360° 3D LiDAR scanner that HOOKII says outputs 10 times more point-cloud data than the first-generation Neomow X. There's no RTK antenna to site and no satellite signal to lose, so trees and buildings shouldn't throw it off. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) explains why LiDAR does well in shade.

Because LiDAR doesn't need open sky, HOOKII encourages putting the charging station indoors. That's a useful theft deterrent that RTK mowers can't match, and it keeps the dock out of the weather. HOOKII says installation takes about five minutes.

How you map depends on the model. Only the X2 Pro auto-maps out of the box, because auto-mapping relies on the triple-vision camera. On the Air, SE and standard X2, you drive the boundary yourself in the app unless you add the $339 module. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) has tips for manual mapping.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

HOOKII rates every X2 for 45% grade (24°) and lists front-wheel drive. That puts it level with mainstream 2WD LiDAR mowers like the Navimow i215, and well short of the 80% AWD crowd. Treat the rating as a best case for dry, short grass, and check your steepest section with the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/).

Obstacle handling is the bigger question. With the camera, HOOKII claims recognition of more than 500 obstacle types. Without it, the cheaper tiers rely on LiDAR and a 110° bumper, which means they detect objects by shape and contact rather than by recognizing them. LiDAR can see a garden gnome but may not see a thin hose or a small toy. We haven't found independent testing of either configuration, so clear the lawn before runs and use no-go zones generously.

## Coverage, cutting and edges

The 11" (28 cm) deck is wide for the price; most mowers under $1,500 cut 7" to 9". HOOKII rates mowing speed at up to 3,200 sq ft per hour and runtime at about 150 minutes per charge (200 on the Pro), covering roughly 0.17 acre per charge (0.25 acre on the Pro). The mower returns to charge and resumes where it stopped. Cutting height adjusts electrically in the app from 1.18" to 3.35", and HOOKII says the cutting system handles St. Augustine, Bermuda, bluegrass and ryegrass.

HOOKII doesn't publish an edge-gap figure. It lists "seamless edge-cutting algorithms" among the features the vision module unlocks, which suggests edge results on the cheaper tiers are basic. Plan on regular string-trimmer passes along fences and beds.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

The HOOKII app handles maps, no-go areas, schedules, cutting height and speed, with three preset mowing modes plus a custom option. Amazon listings cite up to 30 zones on the Air and 90 on the X2. HOOKII listed dual-map storage and cloud map backup as arriving in the app after June 2026, so confirm they're live if you need them.

HOOKII calls its anti-theft five-layer protection: indoor dock placement, a lift alarm, admin-only app access, GPS tracking through Find My Device (with remote locking, per the Amazon listing), and an email with the mower's location if it reconnects after going offline. The listing describes 4G with "1GB free" data, and HOOKII doesn't make clear what continued service costs. See our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) for more.

## Ownership notes

- **Lightly reviewed:** we haven't found a full independent review of the X2. The reviews on HOOKII's own store are uniformly five-star, which isn't a substitute for independent testing.
- **Warranty and returns:** HOOKII's store advertises a two-year warranty and 30-day returns. Buying through Amazon gives you Amazon's return window too.
- **Prices vary by store:** our tier prices are Amazon US, October 2026. HOOKII's own store has listed different tier names and higher prices, so compare before buying.
- **Weather:** rated IPX6, below 58 dB, with a removable battery. HOOKII sells blades, discs and a rain cover.
- **Weight:** about 37 lb (40 lb for the Pro), manageable for one person.

## How the Neomow X2 compares

The [Segway Navimow i215 LiDAR](/mowers/segway-navimow-i215-lidar/) is the obvious rival on smaller lots: also LiDAR, also rated 45%, with an 8.7" deck and a camera as standard, for about $1,399 to $1,599 at 0.37 acre. It costs more than the X2 Air but has well-documented reviews. The [ECOVACS GOAT A LiDAR Pro](/mowers/ecovacs-goat-a-lidar-pro/) covers 0.5 to 0.75 acre with dual LiDAR, a camera, a 50% rating and automatic edge trimming, for about $1,399 to $1,849. That's more than the SE, but it's one of the best-reviewed mowers in our database.

## Bottom line

The Neomow X2 offers big-lawn LiDAR coverage at prices nobody else matches, and the SE at about $1,300 for 0.75 acre is the standout. Go in knowing the limits: gentle slopes only, LiDAR-only obstacle detection unless you pay for the camera, and almost no independent testing yet. For a large, flat, tree-dotted lawn on a budget, it deserves a look.
