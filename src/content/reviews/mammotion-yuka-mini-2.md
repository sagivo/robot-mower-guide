The Mammotion YUKA mini 2 1000H is a capable quarter-acre LiDAR mower: no antenna, strong obstacle avoidance, and setup that reviewers finished in about 10 to 15 minutes. The problem is the competition. At a typical $1,399 to $1,559, it costs about the same as the Segway Navimow i215 LiDAR and Dreame A3 AWD, which include 4G or AWD and have been easier to find in stock. It's a good mower that's hard to recommend over its neighbors.

## Who the YUKA mini 2 is for (and who should skip it)

It suits flat to moderately sloped yards up to about 10,000 sq ft, especially ones with trees, fences or walls that would trouble an RTK mower. It's also a reasonable pick if you're already in the Mammotion app ecosystem, or if you find it on a good sale.

Skip it if:

- **You have slopes steeper than about 45% (24°).** It's rear-wheel drive. Mammotion's own LUBA mini 2 AWD climbs much steeper ground.
- **You want theft tracking included.** 4G is a $129 add-on.
- **You need it this week.** US stock has been patchy, so check availability before planning around it.

## Navigation and setup

The YUKA mini 2 navigates with a 360° LiDAR sensor plus dual-camera AI vision. Mammotion quotes a LiDAR range of about 2,362" (roughly 197 feet) and 0.6" navigation precision. Because it maps by "seeing" the yard rather than listening to satellites, tree cover isn't a problem. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) explains why that matters in shady yards.

EasyLawnMowing's hands-on review found setup took about 10 to 15 minutes and called assembly "refreshingly simple." The reviewer said LiDAR guidance excels in tree-covered areas where GPS signals weaken, and that the mower moved between zones without hesitation. Mapping can be automatic or manual, and Mammotion supports cloud backup of up to 10 lawn maps.

DropMow is a nice extra. You set the mower on an unmapped patch and it mows that area with no map at all, which is handy for a strip by the driveway or a patch outside your mapped zones. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) has general first-run tips.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

Mammotion rates the YUKA mini 2 for 45% slopes (about 24°), using a rear-wheel-drive layout with a 65 W hub motor. That rating, like every manufacturer figure, assumes dry, short grass. On damp grass, EasyLawnMowing advised care with cutting height to avoid scalping. For anything steeper, read our [slope limits explainer](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/).

Obstacle avoidance is the standout. Mammotion claims recognition of more than 300 obstacle types with motion prediction. EasyLawnMowing said the mower picked up small items like gloves and footballs that other models miss, and gave it an overall 89/100, calling it "A Masterclass in LiDAR Navigation." The reviewer also praised its "straight, parallel mowing lines."

## Coverage, cutting and edges

The deck is 7.5" wide, and Mammotion quotes about 2,690 sq ft per charge with up to 2.5 hours of mowing from a 6.1 Ah battery. The full quarter acre will take several cycles. That's normal for this size class.

Do the math before you buy. At about 2,690 sq ft per charge, a full 10,764 sq ft lawn needs roughly four charges per complete pass, plus recharge time between them. That's fine on a robot that mows a little most days, which is how these mowers are meant to work. But if your mowable area is close to the quarter-acre rating, the YUKA mini 2 will be running nearly every day in peak spring growth. A lawn of 6,000 to 8,000 sq ft is the comfortable sweet spot. Our [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) can help you check the fit.

Cutting height is listed as 2.0" to 3.5", though Mammotion's own pages disagree (another lists 2.2" to 4.0"), so check the listing for the exact unit you buy. EasyLawnMowing noted height adjustment on its review unit was manual, on the mower, rather than in the app. On edges, that review reported excellent results near stone borders with little follow-up trimming. Mammotion markets a cleaner edge-mowing mode, but expect to trim fences and raised beds by hand like any wheeled robot.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

The Mammotion app handles scheduling, mowing patterns, no-go zones and up to 10 zones. Connectivity is Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Tracking requires the optional 4G module (about $129), which Mammotion pairs with a three-year free 4G service trial. Without it, you have no remote location tracking. If your yard faces the street, budget for the module. Our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) covers what else helps.

## Ownership notes

- **Weatherproofing.** IP67 is one of the better ratings in this class. See our [rain guide](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/).
- **Stock.** US availability has been spotty. If it's out of stock, the rivals below are good substitutes.
- **Pricing.** Mammotion has listed it at $1,399 on sale versus $1,559 regular. Add $129 if you want 4G.
- **Maintenance.** Razor blades wear quickly on sandy or debris-heavy lawns. See our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/).

## Bottom line

The YUKA mini 2 1000H does the important things well. LiDAR mapping with no antenna, sharp obstacle avoidance and fast setup all earn praise from reviewers. But at about $1,399 to $1,559 (October 2026), it's up against rivals that include 4G or AWD for the same money. Buy it on a solid discount, or if it's in stock when the others aren't. Prices and stock change often.
