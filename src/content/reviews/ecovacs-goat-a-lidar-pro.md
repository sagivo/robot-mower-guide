The ECOVACS GOAT A3000 LiDAR Pro (and the smaller A2000) is the closest any mainstream robot comes to fully hands-off lawn care. Its built-in TruEdge string trimmer handles the border strip every other robot leaves behind, and its dual-LiDAR navigation needs no antenna or satellites. The limits are terrain and speed: it's rear-wheel drive, rated for 50% slopes, and slow to cover its full rated area.

## Who the GOAT A LiDAR Pro is for (and who should skip it)

It's for owners of flat-to-moderate lawns between about a quarter and three-quarters of an acre who hate edging. Reviewed named the A3000 its best overall robot mower after a month on a 0.75-acre, gently hilly yard split by a retaining wall. BGR called it "easily one of the best robotic lawn mowers" while noting it's only worth the price if automated edging is a priority.

Skip it if your yard has steep sections. 50% inside the work area is fine for rolling lawns, but the 20% limit across virtual boundaries rules out mowing between zones on a slope. Skip it if the trimmer's noise would bother neighbors, or if you want strong built-in theft tracking without an add-on.

## Navigation and setup

Navigation pairs a 360° LiDAR scanner with a 3D time-of-flight sensor and an AI camera. With no satellites involved, trees, covered patios and the shadow of your house aren't a problem. BGR called it "easily the best setup experience" of any mower it tried: after scanning a QR code, auto-mapping built a 3D map of the yard in about five minutes, and there's no antenna to place.

LiDAR needs landmarks, so a perfectly open field with nothing around it is less ideal than a typical fenced backyard. Reviewed also noted that the charging dock has no protective roof, even though the LiDAR sits on top of the mower, so a covered spot or an aftermarket garage is worth considering. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) covers dock placement.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

Cut quality earns consistent praise. BGR described "clean and tear-free" cuts in systematic stripes, and Reviewed said the lawn stayed neat throughout its month of testing. BGR reports it climbed inclines up to about 27° (50% grade) on the reviewer's property, matching ECOVACS's rating. Remember that ratings assume dry, short grass, and the 2WD chassis gives you less margin on damp slopes than AWD rivals. Use the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) to see where your steepest spot falls.

The common hiccup is getting hung up. BGR found the mower sometimes misread tall grass or weeds as obstacles and left uncut patches, and admits "sometimes you just need to pick the thing up and move it." It works best on a lawn that's already maintained, not a field of weeds. Picking up sticks and hoses before scheduled runs helps a lot.

## Coverage, cutting and edges

TruEdge is the reason to buy this mower. BGR estimates it "replaced about 75–90% of the manual trimming," though it struggles with tight corners and steep vertical edges. Reviewed called it "great at edging." The trimmer is loud, at about 82 dB compared with about 62 dB for mowing, so don't schedule edging at dawn.

Coverage speed is the hidden trade-off. With a 13" deck, Reviewed measured roughly 1,000 sq ft per 45 minutes. At that pace, a full 0.75-acre lawn takes several days of scheduled runs to cover, which is fine for maintenance mowing but means the A3000 has little headroom at its rated maximum. If your lawn is close to 0.75 acre, consider a faster wide-deck mower or accept a multi-day cycle. The [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) can help.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

The ECOVACS app handles zones, no-go areas and scheduling well. The main annoyance, per BGR, is that mowing and edge trimming are scheduled as separate tasks rather than one job. Rain handling is sensible: BGR says it returns to the dock when rain starts and waits for about three hours of dry weather before resuming. See [do robot mowers work in rain](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/) for why that matters.

Connectivity is 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, with an optional cellular module. ECOVACS doesn't clearly list anti-theft features on its US spec sheet, and owners in the ECOVACS GOAT community have called the alarm weak. If the mower is visible from the street, read our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).

## Ownership notes

- **Consumables:** BGR says the trimmer line needs replacing about every four to six weeks, on a similar schedule to the blades. It's a tool-free, few-minute job, but it's an extra cost.
- **Known issues:** ECOVACS's support pages address trimmer line that fails to auto-feed, rain-sensor problems that stop the mower from returning on wet days, and base-station connection errors. Those are worth knowing about even if you never hit them.
- **Weight:** about 40 lb, per BGR, with a carry handle for when it gets stuck.
- **Pricing:** about $1,399 for the A2000 and $1,849 for the A3000 in October 2026, down from roughly $2,500 at launch. Prices change often.

## Bottom line

If edging is the chore you most want gone and your lawn is flat to gently rolling, the GOAT A3000 LiDAR Pro is the best choice in 2026. BGR rated its setup the best it had seen, LiDAR ignores tree cover, and TruEdge removes most of the trimming. Accept a loud trimmer, slow coverage near its rated maximum, and weak built-in theft protection. If you have real hills, choose an AWD mower instead.
