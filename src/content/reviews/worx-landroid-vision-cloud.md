The WORX Landroid Vision Cloud is the robot mower we'd point a cautious first-time buyer toward if their lawn is flat. It skips both the boundary wire and the RTK antenna, reviewers consistently call the app one of the easiest to use, and it's sold at nearly every big-box store. The tradeoff is a 30% slope limit and short runs per charge, so it isn't the right machine for hills or for owners who want the lawn finished fast.

## Who the Vision Cloud is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if you have a flat or nearly flat lawn between a quarter acre and a full acre, you've never owned a robot mower, and you value an easy return at a store down the road. It's also a natural fit if you already own WORX 20V tools, because the mower uses the same Power Share battery.

Skip it if:

- **Any part of your lawn is steeper than about 30% (17°).** That's a manufacturer figure for dry, short grass. Real-world grip on damp turf is lower. Use our [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) before you commit.
- **You want the lawn done in one session.** The narrow 8.7" deck and roughly 60–80 minute runtime mean this mower works a little every day rather than finishing quickly.
- **Your yard is mostly under trees.** It relies on satellites first. A LiDAR mower such as the [Navimow i215 LiDAR](/mowers/segway-navimow-i215-lidar/) is a safer bet under heavy canopy.

## Navigation and setup

The Vision Cloud combines network RTK, a stereo camera with V-SLAM, and AI obstacle detection. In plain terms, satellites give it centimeter-level position across open lawn, and the camera takes over when tree cover or a wall blocks the sky. WORX's version of network RTK is called RTK Cloud: corrections arrive over 4G or Wi-Fi instead of from a base station you plant in the yard. If you want the background, our [robot mower navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) covers how RTK, LiDAR and vision differ.

Setup is where it shines. Bob Vila named a Vision Cloud model (the WR320) its "Easiest Setup" pick, and its tester said the app and overall experience "felt much less intimidating than many competing robot mowers." The same review notes it was set up and used by owners in their 70s with little difficulty. Reviewed also gave a Vision Cloud model Editor's Choice, singling out that it skips "both the boundary wire and the staked RTK antenna."

Mapping is AI-assisted: the mower follows your lawn edge with its cameras while RTK records its position, then builds the map. Plan on a little cleanup in the app around beds and paths. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) has a checklist for dock placement and first-run tweaks.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

This is the weak spot. The standard Vision Cloud is a two-wheel-drive mower rated for 30% grades, about 17 degrees. Bob Vila's testers found it handled routine mowing well on small and medium properties, but it "struggled with thick grass in certain lawn edges," and they flagged the two-wheel drive as offering less traction than AWD models. Their summary: "Range can be an issue on expansive lots."

That matches what the specs predict. A narrow deck plus a short runtime means the 1-acre tier needs a lot of daily hours to keep up in peak growing season. On a quarter-acre lawn, it'll be comfortably ahead. For anything with real grade, read our [slope limits explainer](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/) and look at the 4WD version instead.

## Coverage, cutting and edges

The cutting range of 1.57" to 3.54" suits most cool-season lawns, though people who keep fescue at 4" will find the ceiling a bit low. Edges depend on your SKU. WORX's Cut-to-Zero offset trimmer is included on some models and sold as an add-on module for others, so check the box contents before you buy if trimming matters to you. Expect to run a string trimmer along fences and raised beds regardless. No wheeled robot gets fully flush against a vertical edge.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

Connectivity is 4G, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, with GPS/4G tracking and an alarm for theft protection. Cellular matters here for two reasons. It delivers the RTK corrections, and it lets the mower report its location if someone walks off with it. WORX advertises the RTK Cloud service and 4G as free with no added cloud costs. That's a meaningful saving over brands that charge for cellular after year one, but confirm the terms at purchase. Our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) explains what tracking can and can't do.

## Ownership notes

- **Retail support is the selling point.** Home Depot, Lowe's, Best Buy and Walmart all carry WORX. If the mower doesn't suit your yard, returning it is far easier than shipping a bulky box back to a direct-only brand.
- **Shared batteries.** The 20V Power Share pack works across WORX's cordless tool line, so a spare battery isn't a single-purpose purchase.
- **Extras cost extra.** The FiatLux night light and Cut-to-Zero module are add-ons on some SKUs, so price the setup you want, not just the base mower.
- **Owner-reported issues** are mostly about capacity rather than reliability: thick or overgrown edges and big lots stretch it. Keep blades sharp (see our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/)).
