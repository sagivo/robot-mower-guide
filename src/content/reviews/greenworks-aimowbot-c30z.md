The Greenworks AiMowbot C30Z is a three-quarter-acre, wire-free robot mower from a brand most people know from big-box tool aisles. It pairs RTK positioning (with an included reference antenna) and AI cameras, runs quietly at 54 dB, adjusts cutting height electronically, and carries a 3-year warranty, all for a mid-range price. On paper it's competent. But it's 2WD, it needs an antenna, and we found no independent reviews, so it's hard to choose over proven alternatives.

## Who the Greenworks AiMowbot C30Z is for (and who should skip it)

The C30Z fits open, flat-to-gently-sloped lawns between a quarter and three-quarters of an acre (up to 32,670 sq ft), with a good view of the sky for the antenna. It also suits buyers who value a familiar brand, a long warranty and in-store returns more than cutting-edge navigation.

Skip it if:

- **Your yard is shaded.** RTK needs open sky, and the cameras are a backup, not a replacement.
- **You have slopes over about 35%.** The 45% (24°) rating is a dry-grass ceiling for a 2WD mower.
- **You want antenna-free setup.** Network-RTK and LiDAR rivals skip the extra hardware.
- **You want a proven mower.** No professional reviews yet, and the sibling C20's buyer ratings are weak.

## Navigation and setup

Greenworks calls the system RTKVision 2.0: RTK satellite positioning corrected by a reference antenna you install, plus smart cameras and night-vision sensors for obstacle detection. Greenworks claims 1-inch navigation accuracy and detection of more than 200 obstacle types.

Setup takes more planning than antenna-free mowers. In the box you get the antenna kit, the charging station, two separate power supplies (29V for the dock, 12V for the antenna) and two 32-ft low-voltage cables. Greenworks' guidance for the antenna is at least 120° of open sky with nothing directly overhead, and an extension kit (sold separately) is available to mount it on a roof or wall. The dock needs level ground (less than about 2 in of drop front to back), a clear 6.5-ft radius, open sky about 3 ft in front, and ideally Wi-Fi coverage for firmware updates.

Mapping is manual. You drive the mower around each mowing area in the app, staying within about 20 ft of it, then add no-go zones and paths linking separate areas to the dock. Greenworks recommends keeping the mower about 4 in inside the real lawn edge while mapping. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) covers antenna and dock placement, and the [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) explains why RTK cares about sky view.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

With two-wheel drive, oversized wheels and a 45% grade rating (about 24°), the C30Z is built for flat to moderately sloped lawns. That's typical for a mid-priced 2WD mower, but it's well short of the 80% ratings on AWD models, including Greenworks' own G Series. Ratings assume dry, short grass, so give yourself a margin on wet mornings. Check your grade with our [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) and see our [slope limits explainer](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/).

Greenworks says night mowing is supported with LED headlights, but its FAQ admits the vision system "may be less sensitive in low light," which can reduce small-object detection and make docking slower. If pets or kids' toys end up on the lawn, schedule mowing for daylight. Our [safety guide](/posts/robot-mower-safety-pets-kids-wildlife/) has more.

## Coverage, cutting and edges

The 8" cutting width is on the narrow side for three-quarters of an acre, and Greenworks lists a 0.9 mph mowing speed and a 50-minute recharge. A full-size lawn will take several cycles, which is normal at this size if you run a daily schedule.

The standout spec is electronic height adjustment from 0.75" to 3.5", set in the app. Few robots go as low as 0.75", which suits Bermuda and other warm-season grasses kept short, while 3.5" covers most cool-season lawns. Greenworks says edge mowing reaches within 4", so expect to trim along fences and beds.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

The app handles mapping, zones, no-go areas, schedules and firmware updates, including optional overnight "silent" updates. Connectivity is Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and 4G. Greenworks' FAQ says 4G is activated during setup and refers to renewing an expired data plan, so budget for a subscription at some point, though pricing isn't published on the product page.

Anti-theft starts with account binding: once the mower is linked to your app account, nobody else can use it. There's also a PIN device lock. With 4G active and anti-theft enabled, the mower sounds an alarm if it's moved out of its area and you can track its location. See our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).

## Ownership notes

- **Warranty:** 3 years on the tool, longer than the 2 years common in this class.
- **Retail:** sold by Greenworks and Walmart, so returns and exchanges are easier than with direct-only brands.
- **Battery:** user-replaceable with genuine Greenworks batteries, which matters for long-term ownership. See [how long robot mowers last](/posts/how-long-do-robot-mowers-last/).

## Bottom line

The Greenworks AiMowbot C30Z is a conventional RTK-plus-camera mower with quiet running, a useful low cutting height and a strong warranty. Against that: it needs an antenna and two power runs, it's 2WD, and nobody has independently tested it. Buy it if easy Walmart returns and a 3-year warranty matter most and your three-quarter-acre lawn is open and gentle. Otherwise, proven rivals are the safer bet.
