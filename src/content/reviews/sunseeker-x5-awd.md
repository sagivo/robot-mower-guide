The Sunseeker Elite X5 AWD is a half-acre, all-wheel-drive robot mower that pairs RTK satellite positioning with camera-based VSLAM. The Register's hands-on review praised its quiet running and neat stripes. At $1,999 for the current Gen 2, though, it costs more than LiDAR AWD rivals that climb steeper slopes and skip the base station. It's a sound mower in a crowded price bracket, so whether it makes sense depends mostly on what you pay.

## Who the Sunseeker X5 is for (and who should skip it)

The X5 suits open, moderately sloped lawns up to about half an acre (21,780 sq ft) with a good view of the sky. The tri-wheel AWD and independent suspension are its real selling points: two driven front wheels plus a steerable driven rear wheel give it more grip than the 2WD mowers that dominate the under-$1,500 market.

Skip it if:

- **Your yard is under heavy tree cover.** RTK needs satellites, and VSLAM only partly fills the gaps. A LiDAR mower like the [Dreame A3 AWD](/mowers/dreame-a3-awd/) doesn't care about canopy.
- **You'd pay full price.** At $1,999, the X5 Gen 2 costs more than the Dreame A3 AWD 2000 (about $1,539), which covers the same half acre and is rated for 80% slopes.
- **You want hands-off theft protection.** The 4G-GPS module is an optional extra.

## Gen 1 vs Gen 2: which X5 are you buying?

Two products share the X5 name, and listings don't always make clear which is which. The original X5 is the one independent reviewers have covered. Sunseeker's US page for it lists an RTK base station, a holder and a separate base-station charger in the box.

The X5 Gen 2 is marketed with AONavi, a "fusion of RTK and VSLAM 2.0," a redesigned binocular plus iToF camera system for day-and-night perception, a 10 TOPS AI chip and a micro-blade edge disc that Sunseeker says cuts within 1.2" of edges. Its US page headlines "Wire-Free, Antenna-Free, Hassle-Free," but it doesn't say whether that means network RTK or list the box contents. We couldn't find an independent review of Gen 2. Until one appears, treat the antenna-free claim as unconfirmed and ask the seller what ships in the box.

## Navigation and setup

On Gen 1, setup means placing the RTK base station where it can see open sky, then walking the mower around each zone's boundary in the app. The Register described zone setup as "a simple case of walking the mower around its boundary and saving the zone." After that, the mower leaves the dock on schedule and mows in neat lines.

The base station was The Register's biggest gripe. The base and the charging dock each need their own power supply, so you need two outlets or two cable runs. The upside is that you can mount the base high, such as on a shed roof, where it gets the best sky view. Sunseeker also told The Register that network RTK would come in future models and that the X5 has no upgrade path. If an antenna-free install matters to you, our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) and [wire-free buying guide](/posts/robot-mower-without-boundary-wire/) explain the trade-offs.

Obstacle avoidance uses the cameras and onboard AI. Sunseeker says the X5 recognizes over 200 obstacle types, and The Register reported it avoided the family cat and toys left on the lawn. You can't stream the camera feed to the app, though, so there's no remote "look around" feature.

## Mowing performance, slopes and terrain

This is where the X5 earns its keep. The Register said the chunky front wheels and single rear steering wheel "made light work of the undulations" in its test lawn. Sunseeker's rear active steering motor turns the mower without scuffing turf, which helps on soft spring grass.

Sunseeker lists climbing as "60% / 30°," but 30° is about a 58% grade, so we use 58%. The Register couldn't verify the rating. Either way it's a solid figure, but it trails the 80% (about 39°) rating of the Dreame A3 AWD. Manufacturer ratings assume dry, short grass, so if your steepest section is near 50%, measure it with our [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) and read our [slope limits explainer](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/) before buying.

## Coverage, cutting and edges

The X5 has an 8.6" floating cutting disc that follows the ground's contours. The Register measured a 22 cm (about 8.7") swath and called the resulting finish and stripes "impressive." Runtime is modest: The Register saw just over an hour per charge, so a full half acre takes several cycles.

The Gen 1 cutting height is set by hand, which The Register called an annoyance. Sunseeker's US page lists 1.6" to 3.2", while The Register's EU unit was listed at 20–60 mm, so check the listing if you keep your grass tall. Gen 2 adds edge-following and "ride-on" boundary modes plus spot cutting for patches missed around obstacles. The 1.2" edge claim is unverified, so keep a trimmer handy until reviews confirm it.

## App, connectivity and anti-theft

The app covers multiple zones, no-go areas, schedules, mowing angles and map edits, and supports Alexa and Google Assistant. Connectivity is Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. For theft protection, Sunseeker sells an optional 4G-GPS module that adds real-time tracking, geofencing and boundary alerts. Without it, your mower depends on whatever lock and alarm features the app provides. Our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) covers what else you can do.

## Ownership notes

- **Rain:** the mower detects rain and returns to the dock. The Register lists the mower as IPX5, so it can be hosed down but not pressure-washed. The Gen 1 base station is IPX4. See [do robot mowers work in rain](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/).
- **Noise:** about 60 dB, quiet enough for daytime mowing without bothering neighbors.
- **Power:** plan for two outdoor outlets on Gen 1, one for the dock and one for the base station.
- **Pricing:** Gen 2 lists at $1,999 on Sunseeker's US site. The Register quoted €1,899 for Gen 1 in Europe. Prices change, and Gen 1 stock is where discounts tend to show up.

## Bottom line

The Sunseeker X5 is a capable AWD mower with a good finish, quiet running and a sensible app. Its problem is the competition: at $1,999 it costs more than LiDAR AWD rivals that climb steeper slopes and need no base station. Buy it if you find a real discount and your lawn has open sky. Otherwise, the Dreame A3 AWD is the better half-acre buy.
