## Why wire-free changed everything

Old boundary-wire mowers worked, but setup was miserable: burying or pegging wire around the whole perimeter, splicing breaks, and hoping the dog didn't chew through it. Wire-free navigation removed the worst part of ownership.

Three technologies do the job now:

- **RTK-GNSS** — satellite positioning with a fixed base station, accurate to centimeters. Works great in open sky; weak under dense canopy.
- **LiDAR** — spinning lasers map the yard in 3D. Excellent precision, works in the dark, costs more.
- **AI vision** — cameras recognize grass edges, obstacles, and even pets. Husqvarna launched seven AI-vision Automower models in late 2025; Mammotion's LUBA 3 AWD (announced January 2026) fuses multiple sensor types.

Most 2026 models combine at least two of these. We explain the tradeoffs in detail in our [RTK vs LiDAR vs camera guide](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/).

## What to check before you buy

**1. Coverage vs. your actual lawn.** Manufacturers rate in "up to" acres. If you're at the edge of a rating, size up — a mower running at 100% duty cycle has no margin for thick spring grass. Our [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) does this math.

**2. Navigation type vs. your yard's cover.** Open sky, few trees: RTK-GPS is fine. Heavy tree canopy or mowing near the house walls: favor LiDAR or vision-heavy models. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) goes deeper.

**3. Slope.** This is the #1 reason people buy the wrong mower. AWD models exist precisely because standard wheels fail on grades. Run our [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) before committing.

**4. Noise and schedule.** Robot mowers are quiet (roughly vacuum-cleaner level or less) and mow a little daily instead of once a week. That's the secret to the stripe-free look: constant micro-trimming, mulched clippings, no bagging.

**5. Theft.** These machines cost $1,000–$3,000 and sit in your yard. Every serious model has GPS tracking and a PIN lock. Check the feature list before buying — cheap models sometimes skip it.

## Wire-free vs. boundary-wire: is the old tech dead?

Not entirely. Boundary-wire mowers are still cheaper, and a well-installed wire system is extremely reliable — the signal can't be blocked by trees. But in 2026 the wire-free models won on convenience, and manufacturers are clearly moving investment there. Unless you're on a strict budget under $500, buy wire-free.

## Setup: what to actually expect

Wire-free setup is genuinely easy, but it's not zero-effort. Plan for 30–60 minutes:

1. **Place the charging station** on flat ground near a power outlet, with clear sky above it (the RTK base station usually lives here).
2. **Charge the mower fully** before first mapping — a half-charged mower dying mid-map wastes the session.
3. **Walk the boundary in the app.** You drive the mower around your lawn's edge with your phone, or let it auto-map. This is the step people rush. Walk it carefully, keep the boundary a foot inside the true edge, and mark keep-out zones around flower beds, the trampoline, and anywhere the mower shouldn't go.
4. **Run a test mow and watch it.** The first run reveals mapping mistakes. Fix them now, not after it eats the petunias.

The most common setup failure is impatience: mapping on a half-charged battery, or drawing a sloppy boundary and wondering why the mower hugs the fence.

## Maintenance: the honest version

Robot mowers are low-maintenance, not no-maintenance:

- **Blades:** swap every 1–3 months in season ($20–$40/year). Five minutes, no tools beyond the included key.
- **Cleaning:** hose off grass buildup monthly. Caked decks reduce cutting quality and strain the motors.
- **Winter:** store indoors, battery at ~50% charge. Mowers left in freezing sheds come back with dead batteries.
- **Software:** install firmware updates. 2025–26 models improved obstacle avoidance noticeably through updates alone.

Total annual maintenance cost for most owners: under $100, plus an hour or two of your time.

## Who shouldn't buy a robot mower

- **Lawns under ~2,000 sq ft that take 20 minutes to push-mow.** The payback math in our [cost breakdown](/posts/robot-mower-vs-lawn-service-cost/) doesn't work when the job is trivial.
- **Yards with constant obstacles** — construction zones, kids' sports practice areas, free-roaming large dogs that treat the mower as a toy. Vision helps, but physics wins.
- **Very narrow side yards** (under 3 feet). No mower navigates a bowling lane well.
- **HOAs with mowing-day rules.** Some HOAs require visible mowing on specific days. A robot mowing daily can technically violate "mow on Tuesday" covenants. Check before buying.
