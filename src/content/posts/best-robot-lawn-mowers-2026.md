## The best wire-free robot mowers, pick by pick

### 1. Segway Navimow X4: best robot lawn mower overall

**Why we picked it:** The X4 does the most important things best for the most yards. Its 17-inch deck is the widest in its class, so it finishes big lawns faster than anything near its price. The 4WD chassis with Xero-Turn steering carries an 84% (about 40°) slope rating, tied for the highest of any wheeled mower we track. Navigation combines network RTK (Segway's EFLS 3.0) with 360° camera VSLAM and visual-inertial odometry, and mapping can be automatic or done by remote control. It comes in two sizes: the X430 covers 1 acre and the X450 covers 1.5 acres, both premium-tier prices. It's also our top-scored model at 9.3/10.

The X4 is designed to run on network RTK without an antenna, but whether your address has good coverage varies. Some yards still need the included RTK antenna, so plan a spot for it just in case. Reviewers also note it occasionally leaves spots that need a trim, and its EdgeSense edge mode can scuff raised bed borders.

**Skip it if:** your lawn is under about half an acre (the smallest tier is rated for a full acre, so you'd be paying for capacity you won't use), or your yard sits under dense tree canopy, where a LiDAR mower like the Dreame A3 AWD Pro holds position more reliably.

### 2. Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD: best for hills

**Why we picked it:** On paper the X4's 84% rating edges out the LUBA 3's 80% (about 39°). But hilly yards rarely come alone. They tend to have trees, rough ground, retaining walls and tight turns too, and that's where the LUBA 3 pulls ahead. Its Tri-Fusion navigation combines 360° LiDAR, network RTK and dual-camera AI vision, so it keeps its position under canopy where RTK-only mowers drift. All-wheel drive plus suspension earns it a perfect 10 for terrain in our scoring, and it needs no base station. The 15.7-inch dual-disc deck mows fast, and 4G is included for three years.

Pricing is unusual. The 3000 tier (0.75 acre) is often discounted below the price of the smaller 1500 tier, so compare before you buy. The 5000 tier covers 1.25 acres at a flagship-tier price. Each tier comes in a Standard version (1.0 to 2.7-inch cut) or an "H" high-cut version (2.2 to 4.0 inches), so pick H if you keep your grass tall.

**Skip it if:** your lawn is flat and open (you're paying for capability you won't use), or you want a light machine. At 41 pounds it can scuff turf on tight turns, and owners report some app lag.

### 3. Dreame A3 AWD Pro: best for trees and LiDAR navigation

**Why we picked it:** If trees, buildings or tall fences block the sky over your lawn, you want a mower that doesn't depend on satellites at all. The A3 AWD Pro navigates with 360° 3D LiDAR plus binocular AI vision (Dreame calls it OmniSense 3.0), with no RTK in the loop. It matches the LUBA 3 on paper with an 80% slope rating, all-wheel drive, active suspension and a 15.8-inch deck, and adds an automatic deck lift. At 65 dB it's reasonably quiet. Its anti-theft kit is the most complete here: PIN, lift alarm, 4G tracking with three years of eSIM service, an AirTag slot and a camera security patrol mode.

The real hook is price. It is frequently on sale well below list, which brings the 2500 (0.62 acre) and 3500 (0.87 acre) tiers down to mid-range prices and the 5000 (1.24 acres) to premium. Gizmodo's review called it a compelling case for LiDAR mowers. Reviewers also note that obstacle avoidance is good but not perfect, and one major review found missed patches.

**The close alternative:** the [MOVA LiDAX Ultra AWD](amazon:mova-lidax-ultra-awd) is essentially the same platform under a different badge (360° LiDAR plus AI vision, AWD, an 80% rating and a 15.8-inch deck), and it has reviewed better so far: SlashGear called it the best robot mower it has tested, and it scores 8.8/10 with us against the Pro's 8.7. The 2000 (0.5 acre) is a mid-range price and the 3000 (0.75 acre) is premium tier, so the Dreame still covers more lawn per dollar at current sale prices. MOVA doesn't publish an IP rating for the AWD models, and its app is less mature. See our [LiDAX Ultra AWD review](/mowers/mova-lidax-ultra-awd/).

**Skip it if:** you want edges done for you (you'll still need a string trimmer), or the sale ends. At full list price, the LUBA 3 AWD is the better value.

### 4. ECOVACS GOAT A3000 LiDAR Pro: best edge trimming

**Why we picked it:** Every other robot leaves a strip of grass along fences, beds and walls that you finish with a string trimmer. The GOAT A-series is the only mainstream line with a true built-in edge string trimmer (TruEdge), which gets it closer to fully hands-off lawn care than anything else. Navigation uses dual LiDAR (360° plus 3D time-of-flight) with an AI camera, so there's no antenna and no satellite dependence. It's also Reviewed's best overall pick. The A2000 covers 0.5 acre and the A3000 covers 0.75 acre, both at mid-range prices.

**Skip it if:** your yard has real hills. It's rear-wheel drive with a 50% (27°) limit inside the work area and just 20% across virtual boundaries. BGR's review notes it occasionally gets stuck and has to be picked up and moved, the trimmer is loud at around 82 dB, and connectivity is Wi-Fi only unless you add the optional cellular module.

Choose the Dreame for steeper grades, trees or lawns up to half an acre.

**Why we picked it:** The X390's 2.5-acre (108,900 sq ft) rating is unmatched among mainstream wire-free mowers (only the tracked Yarbo platform, at a much higher price, rates higher). It navigates with RTK (network or antenna) plus three wide-angle cameras and a time-of-flight sensor, and reviewers found it fast, accurate and able to hold its position under trees. ## How to choose the best robot mower without a boundary wire

Every mower above is wire-free, so the decision comes down to five questions. Our full [how to choose a robot lawn mower](/posts/how-to-choose-a-robot-lawn-mower/) guide goes deeper on each.

[[diagram:navigation]]

1. **How big is your lawn, really?** Measure the mowable area, not the lot. Then size up about 20–30%, because "up to" ratings assume ideal conditions and thick spring growth eats into them. The [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) does this math and picks the right tier.
2. **How steep is your steepest section?** Two-wheel-drive mowers top out around 45–50%. Anything steeper needs AWD rated 70–84%. Use the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) and read our [slope limits guide](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/) before trusting a spec sheet.
3. **How much sky can the mower see?** Open lawns suit network RTK (Navimow X4, i105N). Heavy canopy, tall buildings or narrow side yards favor LiDAR (Dreame, MOVA, Sunseeker S4, LUBA 3). Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) covers the trade-offs, and [best robot mower for complex yards](/posts/best-robot-mower-for-complex-yards/) covers the hardest layouts.
4. **Do you want edges done?** Only the ECOVACS GOAT line trims edges with a string trimmer. Everything else leaves a strip, though some get within about 2 inches.
5. **Will it be visible from the street?** Prefer 4G tracking. Our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) explains what actually deters thieves.

## What changed in 2026: four trends that shaped these picks

**LiDAR went mainstream.** Thirteen of the 30 models we track now use LiDAR, and the ANTHBOT M5 LiDAR brings it into the budget tier. Two years ago it was a flagship-only feature. LiDAR maps the yard directly, so it works under trees and at dusk, which RTK can't always do.

**Base stations are disappearing.** Network RTK (which uses correction data over the cellular network) and LiDAR have made the rooftop antenna optional. Twenty-one of our 30 models ship without a mandatory base station. The holdouts are Husqvarna's EPOS Automowers, the Lymow One Plus, the ANTHBOT M9, the Yarbo, the RTK version of the Roborock RockMow X1, the Airseekers TRON, the Sunseeker X5 and the Greenworks AiMowbot C30Z. Some network-RTK mowers, including the X4 and X3, may still need their included antenna if coverage at your address is weak.

**AWD reached the budget tier.** The Navimow i2 AWD starts in the budget tier, and the Dreame A3 AWD costs only a little more on sale. A year ago, all-wheel-drive traction was a premium-only feature.

**Edge trimming finally arrived.** ECOVACS's TruEdge trimmer, Mammotion's edge disc on the LUBA mini 2, Dreame's EdgeMaster and WORX's Cut-to-Zero add-on all attack the last chore robots used to leave you. Only the GOAT's string trimmer truly reaches the edge, but the gap is closing.

## Setup and ownership: what to expect

Wire-free setup is easy but not zero-effort, so plan on 30–60 minutes. Put the dock on flat ground near an outlet with decent cellular signal (and a clear sky view if your mower uses an RTK antenna). Charge fully before mapping, keep drive-around boundaries slightly inside the true edge, mark no-go zones around beds and play sets, and watch the first run to catch mapping mistakes. Our [robot mower setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) covers each brand's quirks.

Maintenance is light but real. Blades typically need swapping every one to three months in season (often $20–$40 a year), the deck needs a monthly rinse to clear grass buildup, and the mower should winter indoors with the battery around half charged. Install firmware updates, since 2025–26 models have improved obstacle avoidance through software alone. Budget for 4G too: the LUBA 3 AWD and Dreame A3 AWD Pro include three years, while the Navimow i2 AWD charges about $33 a year after the first. See our [robot mower maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/) and [how long robot mowers last](/posts/how-long-do-robot-mowers-last/).

## Who shouldn't buy a robot mower

- **Tiny lawns.** If it takes 20 minutes to push-mow under about 2,000 sq ft, the payback math in our [robot mower vs lawn service cost](/posts/robot-mower-vs-lawn-service-cost/) breakdown doesn't work.
- **Yards with constant obstacles.** Construction zones, daily sports practice or large dogs that treat the mower as a toy will frustrate even the best obstacle avoidance. Read our [pets, kids and wildlife safety guide](/posts/robot-mower-safety-pets-kids-wildlife/) first.
- **Very narrow passages.** Side yards under about 3 feet wide are hard for any mower to navigate reliably.
- **Strict HOA mowing rules.** Some covenants dictate mowing days or hours. A robot that mows a little every day may not comply, so check first.
- **Big, rough acreage on a budget.** Past 2.5 acres, a [riding mower](/posts/robot-mower-vs-riding-mower/) may still make more sense.
