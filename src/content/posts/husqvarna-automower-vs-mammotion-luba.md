The **Husqvarna Automower vs Mammotion LUBA** choice comes down to what you want to pay for. Husqvarna sells a mature platform, a 4-year warranty and a dealer you can drive to. Mammotion sells newer navigation, all-wheel drive and roughly double the slope rating for less money. For most steep, wooded or cluttered yards, the [Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD](amazon:mammotion-luba-3-awd) is the better machine; for open, gentle lawns where service and longevity matter most, the Husqvarna Automower iQ is the safer bet.

Price comparisons below are based on October 2026 street and campaign prices, and Husqvarna's in particular swing between campaign and list. Check today's prices before you buy.

## The 30-second answer

- **Buy the [Husqvarna Automower iQ](amazon:husqvarna-automower-iq)** (410, 420 or 440 iQ) if your lawn is open and fairly flat, you plan to keep the mower for a decade, you want a local dealer to handle repairs, or you have up to 2 acres of open grass.
- **Buy the Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD** if your yard has slopes steeper than about 24°, lots of trees, or tight obstacles, or if you'd rather spend less for more capable hardware.
- **Small, steep yard?** Skip both flagships and look at the [LUBA mini 2 AWD](/mowers/mammotion-luba-mini-2-awd/).
- **Steep and want Husqvarna anyway?** The Automower 435 iQ AWD exists, but it costs far more than a LUBA 3 AWD 5000.

## Navigation: EPOS and radar vs Tri-Fusion

Husqvarna's **EPOS** (Exact Positioning Operating System) is satellite RTK with a reference station you mount on your property. It's accurate in open sky, and Husqvarna has a fallback most rivals don't: in yards with poor sky view, the iQ can run on a boundary wire instead. The reference station needs its own power outlet, which can mean an extension cord or an electrician. Gear Diary's reviewer notes that Husqvarna's EPOS Cloud service can eliminate the local station where coverage exists, so ask your dealer what applies at your address.

Obstacle detection is where the Automower shows its age. The iQ uses radar rather than cameras or LiDAR. Gear Diary's review of the 420 iQ, titled "The Safe Bet in Robotic Mowing," found the radar outclassed by LiDAR and AI-camera systems. The mower also hung up on roots and logged more errors than other mowers the reviewer tested that year, including slipping out of bounds and wheel-motor-blocked faults.

Mammotion's **Tri-Fusion** system on the LUBA 3 AWD combines 360° LiDAR, network RTK (corrections over 4G, so no base station) and dual AI cameras. TechAeris's Alex Hernandez scored it 9.1/10 and credited the fusion with holding position under tree canopy where GPS-only mowers fail. The same Gear Diary site that called the 420 iQ the safe bet called the LUBA 3 AWD "the McLaren of robot mowers" and its new favorite. The LUBA mini 2 AWD drops RTK and runs on LiDAR and cameras alone, which suits small yards boxed in by trees and buildings.

**Our take:** Husqvarna navigation is dependable in open lawns and a generation behind under trees and around clutter. That's reflected in our navigation subscores: 7.5 for the iQ versus 9.5 for the LUBA 3. For a deeper primer, see [robot mower navigation explained](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/).

## Terrain and slopes: the biggest gap

This is where the matchup stops being close.

The 410, 420 and 440 iQ are **rear-wheel drive and rated for 45% (24.2°) inside the work area, but only 15% (8.5°) at the boundary**. That boundary figure catches people out: if your lawn edge runs along a sloped bank, the mower may not be able to track it. Owners also report RWD traction issues and boundary errors.

The **LUBA 3 AWD and LUBA mini 2 AWD are rated for 80% (38.7°)**. Both use all-wheel drive, and Hernandez found the mini 2 crossed roots and uneven ground without losing traction. The trade-off: the LUBA 3's torque can scuff thin, damp turf on tight turns.

Husqvarna's answer for hills is the **Automower 435 iQ AWD**, an articulated all-wheel-drive design rated 70% (35°) inside and 50% at the boundary. It's a proven platform and copes well with rough ground, but it's priced in the flagship tier, tops out at a 2.8" cut, and still needs the reference station.

All slope ratings assume dry, short grass, so leave a safety margin. ## Cutting quality and edges

**Deck width** favors Mammotion by a wide margin: 15.7" on the LUBA 3 versus 9.4" on the Automower iQ and 8.7" on the 435 iQ AWD. A wider deck needs fewer passes, which matters on bigger lots. Husqvarna's approach is the classic Automower one: small pivoting razor blades mowing little and often, which produces a clean, mulched finish. Gear Diary praised the 420 iQ's clean cut.

**Cutting height** is close on the 2WD models. The Automower iQ runs 1.0–4.0" in one model. The LUBA 3 splits into Standard (1.0–2.7") and H (2.2–4.0") versions, so cool-season lawns kept at 3"+ need the H. The 435 iQ AWD's 2.8" ceiling is a real limitation for fescue or bluegrass in summer.

**Edges:** none of these trims edges automatically, but the LUBA mini 2 AWD comes closest with a dedicated 4.7" side edge disc that cuts to about 2.1" from walls. Hernandez said it significantly reduced string trimming, though its fixed height can leave a slight height difference along borders. Everyone else should plan on regular trimming.

## App, cellular and anti-theft

All four have cellular connectivity and theft tracking, so none are naked to thieves. Husqvarna's Automower Connect app offers GPS tracking, an alarm, PIN and geofencing. Mammotion includes **3 years of 4G data** on both LUBAs, plus live camera view, which Husqvarna's radar-based iQ can't offer.

App feel differs. Husqvarna's app covers the essentials: schedules, stay-out zones, cut height and tracking. Mammotion's app offers more granular control, but TechAeris noted occasional lag on the LUBA 3, and Hernandez advised new users to go through the LUBA mini 2's setup carefully because of the many menus. ## Support and warranty: Husqvarna's trump card

This is why people pay the Husqvarna premium.

- **Track record:** Husqvarna has made robotic mowers since 1995, which Gear Diary cites as a key reason to trust the platform for a decade of ownership.
- **Dealers:** Husqvarna sells through a nationwide dealer network and big retailers like Lowe's and Tractor Supply. Many dealers offer paid installation and map setup (ask yours), and when something breaks you can take it to a shop instead of shipping parts.
- **Warranty:** the iQ series carries a **4-year warranty**, which Gear Diary called best in class.

Mammotion backs the LUBA 3 AWD with a **3-year warranty** (per TechAeris) and handles support direct-to-consumer: app diagnostics, firmware fixes, email and shipped parts. That works well for many owners, but it puts more of the troubleshooting on you. If you're not comfortable swapping a blade disc or wheel motor yourself, weigh this heavily. ## Price and value

Our value subscores put the LUBA 3 AWD, LUBA mini 2 AWD and Automower iQ level at 7.5 each, with the Automower 435 iQ AWD well behind at 5.5. That tie needs context. The iQ earns it at Husqvarna's October campaign prices, which run well below list, and only on open, gentle lawns. At list price, or on a yard with slopes and trees, the Mammotions deliver far more mower per dollar. If you go Husqvarna, time your purchase to a campaign.

The 435 iQ AWD is the hardest to justify on specs. At a flagship-tier price it's one of the most expensive per acre of any model we track, and a LUBA 3 AWD 5000 climbs steeper ground and cuts taller for far less. You're paying for the dealer and the warranty, not the hardware.

## Which should you buy?

### Buy the Husqvarna Automower iQ if...

- Your lawn is open, mostly flat and has a good view of the sky
- You have 1.25 to 2 acres of open grass (the 440 iQ)
- A local dealer, in-person repairs and a 4-year warranty matter more than specs
- You plan to own the mower for 8 to 10 years and value a mature platform

**Skip it if** any part of your lawn is steeper than about 24°, your boundary runs along a slope, or your yard is full of roots and clutter that radar handles poorly.

### Buy the Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD if...

- Your yard has real slopes, up to the 80% rating, or rough, rooty ground

**Skip it if** you need more than 1.25 acres, or if your lawn is flat and simple enough that the cheaper 420 iQ does the job with better local support.

## Final verdict

For most US yards, the **Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD** is the better mower. It climbs nearly twice the grade of the Automower iQ, sees obstacles with LiDAR and cameras instead of radar, needs no reference station, and costs about the same or less at most coverage levels. On small, steep lots, the LUBA mini 2 AWD extends that advantage.

The **Husqvarna Automower iQ** earns its place for a specific buyer: open, gentle lawns, up to 2 acres, owned by someone who values a dealer and a 4-year warranty over cutting-edge navigation. Choose it for the support, not the specs.
