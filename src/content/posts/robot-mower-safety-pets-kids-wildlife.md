Yes, robot mowers are safe for people and pets when used sensibly, and they're far less dangerous than a push or riding mower. They use small pivoting razor blades with low cutting energy, stop their blades within moments of being lifted or tilted, and the better models see obstacles with LiDAR or cameras before touching them. Their real weak spot is small wildlife at night, which is why the single most important safety setting is a daylight-only schedule.

Below: how each safety layer works, where the gaps are, and the rules that make a robot mower a non-issue for families, dogs and wildlife.

## How robot mower blades differ from a gas mower's

A gas push mower or lawn tractor spins one rigid steel bar at high speed, powered by an engine that can hurl rocks and take off toes. A robot mower is a very different machine. Most wire-free models, including everything in our [robot mower comparison chart](/mowers/), use a spinning disc fitted with three or more small razor-style blades that are each held on by a single screw and **pivot freely**.

That pivot matters. When a blade hits something hard, like a root, a toy or a finger, it swings back rather than driving through. Combined with low motor power (these are small battery machines, not 20 hp engines) and a deck that sits recessed under a plastic shell, the cutting energy is a fraction of a gas mower's. That's also why they can't handle tall, overgrown grass: they're designed to trim a little, often.

Pivoting blades are still sharp, and a hand pushed under a running mower can be cut. But the design shifts the risk from "catastrophic" to "avoidable with basic rules."

## Lift, tilt and bump sensors: the safety basics

Every credible robot mower layers several sensors that stop the blades automatically:

- **Lift sensor.** Raise the body and the blades stop. This is the most important protection against curious kids and pets.
- **Tilt and rollover sensors.** If the mower tips past a set angle, for example sliding sideways on a slope, the blades stop and the mower halts.
- **Collision or bump sensors.** The shell floats on the chassis. A hard bump registers, and the mower stops, backs up and turns.
- **Stop button and PIN.** A big red stop button on top halts everything, and a PIN code keeps kids (and thieves) from restarting it. See our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) for the lock and alarm features.

Treat these as the floor, not the ceiling. They react *after* contact or lifting. What separates the best mowers is what they do before contact.

## Obstacle detection compared: LiDAR vs vision vs radar vs bump

Your mower's navigation hardware decides how early it sees an obstacle. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) covers the positioning side; here's how each technology does at not running into things.

On paper, LiDAR plus cameras is the strongest combination, and reviewers back that up: Reviewed named the [ECOVACS GOAT A3000 LiDAR Pro](amazon:ecovacs-goat-a-lidar-pro) its best overall pick partly for obstacle avoidance. Still, no system is perfect. Even the GOAT occasionally gets stuck and needs to be picked up and moved, as BGR's review notes, and the Dreame A3 AWD Pro's avoidance is described by reviewers as "good but not perfect." Assume the mower *might* touch something small, and plan around it.

Also note that a dog lying still in tall grass or a hose coiled in shade is a harder case for AI vision than a standing adult.

## Robot mower safety with pets

Most dogs and cats bark at or chase the mower for a while, then lose interest. The real risks are specific and manageable:

1. **A pet attacking the mower.** Determined chewers can damage sensors or flip a small mower. Tilt and lift sensors stop the blades, but supervise the first few weeks.
2. **A pet sleeping in the mowing path.** This is where detection quality matters. A LiDAR or camera model should steer around a lying dog. A bump-only model will nudge it first.
3. **Pet waste.** Few robots reliably avoid dog poop, and running through it makes a mess of the deck and wheels. Pick up before scheduled runs. Our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/) covers cleaning the underside.
4. **Small pets loose on the lawn.** Rabbits, guinea pigs or tortoises let out to graze are effectively "small wildlife" to a mower. Never run it while they're out.

The simplest rule: schedule mowing for times your pets are indoors, like mid-morning while you're at work. Most apps let you set multiple weekly windows, and many let you pause a run instantly from your phone.

## Robot mowers and kids

Robot mowers remove the biggest danger in home mowing for children. CPSC staff reviewing riding-mower incidents identified almost 200 runover or backover cases and more than 500 rollovers reported from 2011 to 2021, including injuries to children. A robot has no driver who can't see behind them and no seat to ride on. (We cover the riding mower comparison in depth in our [robot mower vs riding mower guide](/posts/robot-mower-vs-riding-mower/).)

That doesn't make it a toy. The risks are a child sitting on it, lifting it by the edge or poking hands underneath. Lift sensors stop the blades, but don't rely on that alone:

- **Don't overlap playtime with mowing time.** Schedule runs for school hours, naps or early afternoon.
- **Turn on the child lock or PIN** so buttons on the mower don't start it.
- **Teach three rules:** don't touch it, don't sit on it, and tell an adult if it gets stuck.
- **Clear toys before runs.** Small plastic toys and balls are the most common reason mowers get stuck, and a blade strike can throw fragments.
- **Turn it off before handling.** Press the stop button before you lift, clean or flip the mower. Wear gloves to change blades.

We couldn't find reliable US injury statistics specifically for robotic mowers. CPSC's published mower data covers walk-behind and riding mowers rather than robots, so treat any precise "robot mower injury" figures you see online with caution.

## Robot mower hedgehogs and wildlife: the real weak spot

This is where robot mowers have a genuine problem. In a 2021 study published in *Animals*, Dr Sophie Lund Rasmussen (now at the University of Oxford) and colleagues tested **18 robot mower models** against dead European hedgehogs collected from rescue centers. None detected juvenile hedgehogs under 200 g without touching them, all models had to make physical contact to detect a hedgehog, and some caused severe injuries while others caused none. Pivoting blades, skid plates and front-wheel drive were associated with safer results.

That research tested mostly older bump-and-wire machines, and today's LiDAR and camera mowers should perform better on larger animals. But no consumer mower we track is certified as wildlife-safe, and small animals that freeze or curl up are exactly what sensors miss. In 2024 Oxford researchers developed a standardized hedgehog safety test in the hope of a future "hedgehog-friendly" certification. It isn't a buying label yet.

There are no wild hedgehogs in the US, but the same logic applies to the animals that are: cottontail nests hidden in lawns, toads, frogs, box turtles, snakes and ground-nesting birds. Many are most active at dusk and overnight.

What the experts advise:

- **Mow in daylight only.** Hedgehog Street, the hedgehog campaign run by the British Hedgehog Preservation Society and People's Trust for Endangered Species, advises running robot mowers "only during the day" and checking the lawn for vulnerable wildlife before starting.
- **Avoid dusk and dawn too.** In April 2026, twelve German animal welfare and conservation groups, in a statement published by the German Animal Welfare Federation, called for a nationwide ban on robot mowing from one hour before sunset to one hour after sunrise. Cities including Cologne, Leipzig, Augsburg and Bayreuth already restrict night use.
- **Use no-go zones** around brush piles, hedges, woodpiles and garden edges where animals shelter.
- **Raise the cutting height** in spring when nests and young animals are most likely.

Some budget mowers are marketed as quiet enough for night mowing. Quiet is good for neighbors, not for wildlife. Set your schedule to daytime regardless of the mower.

## What safety standards robot mowers meet

Robot mowers have their own international safety standard: **IEC 60335-2-107**, "Particular requirements for robotic battery powered electrical lawnmowers." It covers the significant hazards of robotic mowers and their charging stations under normal use and reasonably foreseeable misuse, for battery voltages up to 75 V DC. In North America it's adopted as **ANSI/OPEI 60335-2-107**, harmonized with the CSA and UL editions of the same number (UL 60335-2-107).

Among other things, the standard includes a foot-probe test: a probe shaped like a foot is pushed against the mower all the way around to check that it can't reach the blade path. It also sets requirements for sensors and for preventing inadvertent foot access to the cutting means. Batteries are typically tested separately to **IEC 62133**.

In practice, look for a UL, ETL or CSA certification mark on the mower or in the listing. If an unknown marketplace brand can't tell you which standard it meets, skip it. For comparison, walk-behind and riding mowers fall under a different standard, ANSI/OPEI B71.1.

## Family safety rules: the 8-point checklist

1. **Schedule daytime runs only**, ideally mid-morning to late afternoon.
2. **Don't mow while young kids or pets are on the lawn.** Use app pause or the stop button if plans change.
3. **Enable the PIN and child lock.**
4. **Walk the lawn before the first run of the day** in spring and early summer, looking for toys, nests and animals.
5. **Set no-go zones** around play equipment, sandboxes, dog runs, ponds and brush.
6. **Stop the mower before lifting, cleaning or changing blades.** Gloves on for blade swaps.
7. **Keep blades sharp and the deck clean**, since dull blades tear grass and worn parts make the mower work harder. See [robot mower maintenance](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/).
8. **Respect slope ratings.** Manufacturer ratings assume dry, short grass. If your mower slides on wet hills, it's not safe there. Check yours with the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/).
