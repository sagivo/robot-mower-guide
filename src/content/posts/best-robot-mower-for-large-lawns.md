## Best robot mower for 1 acre

A one-acre lawn is 43,560 sq ft. With 15–20% headroom, you want a mower rated for about 1.15 to 1.2 acres, which is why the 1.25-acre flagships below fit a full acre better than a mower rated for exactly one.

### Segway Navimow X430: best overall for about an acre

**Why we picked it:** The X4 is our best overall pick for typical US lawns from half an acre up, and the X430 is the 1-acre version at about $2,499. The 17" deck is the widest in its class, so it simply covers ground faster than rivals. It adds 4WD with an 84% (40°) slope rating, Xero-Turn steering, easy auto or remote-control mapping and reliable obstacle avoidance. Navigation is network RTK plus 360° vision, designed to run without an antenna.

**Skip it if:** your lawn is a full acre or more. At exactly 1 acre of rated coverage, the X430 has no headroom, so step up to the X450. Also skip it for yards under heavy tree canopy, where LiDAR rivals hold position better, and note that some addresses still need the included RTK antenna. Reviewers report it occasionally misses spots that need a trim.

### Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD 5000: best for large, steep lawns

Tri-Fusion navigation (360° LiDAR, network RTK and dual cameras) means no base station and no dead zones under trees, and AWD with an 80% (38.7°) rating handles hills that strand two-wheel mowers. The 15.7" dual-disc deck mows quickly.

**Why we picked it:** The X390's 2.5-acre rating is unmatched among mainstream wire-free mowers, and it's the clear pick for a robot mower for 2 acres. ## Rated coverage vs real coverage

The area on the box is the maximum lawn a mower can maintain on the maker's recommended schedule, usually mowing most days of the week, in good conditions. It isn't what it cuts on one charge, and it isn't what it'll manage on your lot. Some makers say so openly:

[[chart:value]]

- **Husqvarna** rates the 410/420/440 iQ at 0.5/1/2 acres for open, systematic layouts and says irregular yards get roughly half that. The 435 iQ AWD is rated 1.3 acres systematic and 0.9 acre irregular.
- **Lymow** rates the One Plus per day (1.1 or 1.73 acres) but per charge it covers about 0.57 acre.
- **Most others** publish a single "up to" figure.

Things that cut real coverage: slopes (climbing drains the battery), many obstacles and trees (more turns and avoidance maneuvers), narrow strips and many zones (more transit time between areas), thick spring growth, and wet days skipped by rain delay. A simple open rectangle gets close to the rating. A wooded, hilly, many-bed lot can get well under it.

## Deck width vs mowing time

On a small lawn, deck width barely matters. On a big one, it's the difference between a mower that keeps up in May and one that falls behind. Here's how far each mower must drive to cut one acre once, assuming no overlap (real mowers overlap, so actual distance is higher):

| Deck width | Example | Distance to cut 1 acre once |
|---|---|---|
| 17" | Navimow X4 | ~5.8 miles |
| 16" | Lymow One Plus | ~6.2 miles |
| 15.7"–15.8" | LUBA 3 AWD, Dreame A3 AWD Pro | ~6.3 miles |
| 14" | Sunseeker X7 Gen 2 | ~7.1 miles |
| 9.3"–9.4" | Navimow X3, Automower iQ | ~10.5–10.6 miles |
| 8.7" | Landroid Vision Cloud 4WD | ~11.4 miles |

At the same ground speed, the X4's 17" deck covers about 83% more grass per pass than a 9.3" deck. Narrow-deck mowers make up for it by running more hours and more days, which is why their acreage ratings can still be high, but they have less slack when growth spikes or rain cancels runs. If your big lawn grows fast in spring, favor a wide deck.

## Multi-zone maps and big-yard layout

Large properties rarely form one tidy rectangle. Expect a front yard, a back yard, side strips and maybe a pasture edge. Before you buy, check:

[[diagram:yard-map]]

- **Multi-zone mapping.** You'll want separate zones with their own schedules, cutting heights and mowing directions, connected by paths (often called channels) the mower can drive between them.
- **Crossings.** Driveways, paths and gravel between zones are normal for wire-free mowers, but sketch your route and make sure the crossing is smooth and the mower can reach it.
- **Signal coverage.** RTK mowers can lose their fix in the shadow of a house or tree line between zones, while LiDAR mowers like the Dreame A3 AWD Pro don't depend on satellites. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) covers the differences, and our [complex-yard picks](/posts/best-robot-mower-for-complex-yards/) focus on these layouts.
- **Dock placement.** On a big lot, a central dock cuts transit time. It also needs power and, for RTK models, open sky. The [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) walks through placement.
- **Anti-theft.** A mower working a far corner of a big property, out of sight, needs 4G tracking and alarms. All the picks above offer GPS or 4G tracking; see our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).

## Why you should buy 15–20% headroom

Buying a mower rated for exactly your lawn size leaves no slack for the things that always happen: a wet week, a growth spurt, a new flower bed that adds turns, or a battery that holds a bit less charge after a few seasons. A mower running at its limit falls behind, and a robot that falls behind leaves long grass that is harder for it to cut.

[[diagram:lawn-sizes]]

Our rule: buy at least 15–20% more rated area than your mowable lawn (not your lot size), and more if the yard is hilly or irregular.

| Mowable lawn | Buy a mower rated for at least | Picks that fit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 acre (43,560 sq ft) | 1.15–1.2 ac | LUBA 3 AWD 5000, A3 AWD Pro 5000, Navimow X450 |
| 1.25 acres | 1.44–1.5 ac | Navimow X450, X7 Plus Gen 2, Navimow X350 |
| 1.5 acres | 1.72–1.8 ac | Navimow X390, Automower 440 iQ (open layouts only) |
| 2 acres | 2.3–2.4 ac | Navimow X390 |

Measure your actual turf, not the deed. Subtract the house, driveway, beds and woods, and many "two-acre lots" turn out to have an acre of grass.
