Do robot mowers work in the rain? Physically, yes: they're built to live outside, most 2026 models are rated IPX6 against strong water jets, and a shower won't hurt them. In practice, most are set to **stop** when it rains, because mowing wet grass is bad for your lawn, bad for traction and bad for the cutting deck. The right setup is a rain sensor that sends the mower home, a delay of a few hours before it resumes, and a dock that's protected from lightning and standing water.

## Can robot mowers get wet? The short answer

Yes. A robot mower spends its life outdoors, in dew, sprinklers and summer storms. Manufacturers seal the electronics, battery and motors against water, and most units can be rinsed with a garden hose (check your manual first). What they're **not** designed for:

- **Submersion.** A dock in a low spot that floods can drown the mower. Only the IP67-rated model in our database is built for brief immersion.
- **Pressure washers.** IPX6 is tested with a strong jet at a set distance. A pressure washer at close range is far harsher and can force water past seals. Use a brush and a damp cloth instead.
- **Being flipped upside down in rain.** The deck and underside aren't meant to hold standing water.

So the real question isn't whether the mower survives rain. It's whether it should be mowing while it's raining.

## Robot mower IP ratings explained

An IP (Ingress Protection) code has two digits. The first is protection from solids like dust, from 0 to 6. The second is protection from water, from 0 to 8 (plus 9K for high-pressure washdown). An "X" means that category wasn't rated.

| Rating | Dust | Water | What it means for a mower |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPX4 | Not rated | Splashing from any direction | Fine in rain; least margin of the group |
| IPX5 | Not rated | Water jets (6.3 mm nozzle) | Handles rain and a gentle hose rinse |
| IPX6 | Not rated | Powerful water jets (12.5 mm nozzle) | The 2026 standard; tolerates heavy rain and hosing |
| IP66 | Dust-tight | Powerful water jets | Same water rating as IPX6, plus certified dust protection |
| IP67 | Dust-tight | Temporary immersion (up to 1 m for 30 min) | Survives a puddle or brief flooding |

A higher number means more margin, not permission. An IP67 mower still shouldn't be cutting soaked grass.

## Rain sensors and rain delay settings

Most 2026 wire-free mowers have a rain sensor (often a pair of contacts on the top shell), and some also pull local weather data through the app. When the sensor gets wet, the mower stops and heads back to the dock. Mammotion lists a rain sensor on the LUBA 3, for example.

The important setting is the **rain delay**: how long the mower waits after the rain stops before going back out. WORX's documentation for the Landroid Vision gives a default of **180 minutes, starting once the sensor dries**, and you can change or disable it in the app. Other brands use similar defaults; check yours.

How to set it:

- **Default (2 to 4 hours):** right for most lawns. Grass surfaces dry, soil firms up a little.
- **Longer (4 to 8 hours or next day):** heavy clay soil, slopes, shaded lawns that stay damp, or if you see ruts.
- **Shorter or off:** sandy, fast-draining soil in a wet climate where the mower would otherwise never get out. Owners in very wet climates sometimes do this and raise the cutting height to compensate.

**Dew is not rain.** Most sensors won't trigger on heavy morning dew, but the grass is still wet. If you can, schedule mowing for late morning through afternoon rather than dawn. A wet sensor that won't clear is also a common reason a mower refuses to start; our [troubleshooting guide](/posts/robot-mower-problems-troubleshooting/) covers that.

## Why mowing wet grass is bad for your lawn and traction

Even if the mower is fine, the lawn isn't. Mowing wet grass causes four problems:

1. **Ragged cuts.** Wet blades bend instead of slicing cleanly, so tips tear. Torn tips brown and are more prone to fungal disease.
2. **Clumping.** Wet clippings stick together and to the underside of the deck. Robot mowers mulch fine clippings, and clumps don't mulch; they smother grass. Buildup under the deck also unbalances blades and drains the battery faster. Reviewers specifically note this on the LUBA mini 2 AWD.
3. **Ruts and compaction.** A mower following similar paths over saturated soil leaves tracks, especially heavy AWD models (the LUBA 3 AWD is 41 lb) and especially on turns. The LUBA 3's known con of scuffing turf on tight turns gets worse on wet ground.
4. **Lost traction.** Wheels slip, the mower spins in place, and navigation can drift because wheel odometry no longer matches actual movement.

None of this is a dealbreaker. It's why the rain sensor exists, and why you shouldn't disable it unless you have a good reason. Our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/) covers cleaning wet buildup from the deck and swapping blades.

## Slopes plus wet grass: where mowers get stuck

Every manufacturer slope rating assumes dry, short grass. Wet grass is the single biggest reason a mower that handles a hill in July gets stuck on it in April.

Rough rule: knock a good chunk off the published rating when the grass is damp. A 2WD mower like the [Navimow i105N](/mowers/segway-navimow-i105n-i110n/) (rated 30%, about 17°) or the [eufy E15/E18](/mowers/eufy-e15-e18/) (32%, about 18°) has little margin to begin with. Owners report the eufy slipping on damp grass, and Segway's budget i-series is noted for struggling in mud.

AWD gives you the most headroom. The [Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD](amazon:mammotion-luba-3-awd) is rated 80% (about 39°), the [Segway Navimow X4](amazon:segway-navimow-x4) 84% (about 40°), and the smaller [Segway Navimow i2 AWD](amazon:segway-navimow-i2-awd) 45% (about 24°). If your lawn has a slope and your climate is wet, buy for the wet case. ## Lightning, thunderstorms and power surges

Rain is fine. Lightning isn't. A strike nearby can send a surge through your home's wiring into the charging station, or through the ground into a docked mower.

Husqvarna's support guidance is specific: disconnect the power supply before a thunderstorm, and for its EPOS reference stations, make sure the station isn't mounted higher than a nearby lightning rod. That's reasonable advice for any brand's dock and RTK antenna. Practical steps:

- **Plug the dock into a GFCI outdoor outlet,** which electrical code generally requires anyway, and add an outdoor-rated surge protector.
- **Unplug before forecast storms** if the outlet is easy to reach. Some owners use a smart outdoor plug so they can cut power from their phone.
- **Send the mower home early.** If a storm is coming, pause the schedule in the app.
- **Mount RTK antennas sensibly.** An antenna on a tall pole or the roof peak is the highest point around. Mount it where the manufacturer recommends, not higher.

Lightning damage may not be covered by a standard warranty, so check your manufacturer's terms and your homeowner's policy.

## Garages, covers and where to put the dock

A robot mower can live outside all season, but shelter helps:

- **Dock covers ("mower garages").** A simple roof over the dock keeps rain, sun and leaves off the mower. It protects the plastic shell from UV and keeps the rain sensor from getting soaked while charging. Leave enough clearance for the mower to enter and exit, and keep metal roofs away from RTK antennas and cameras.
- **High, well-drained dock placement.** Never put the dock in a low spot where water pools. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) covers placement in detail.
- **Winter storage indoors.** In freezing climates, bring the mower in for the off-season, charge the battery to the level your manual recommends, and store it dry. Our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/) and [how long robot mowers last](/posts/how-long-do-robot-mowers-last/) cover storage and battery life.

If you're choosing a model and your dock will be fully exposed in a rainy climate, an IPX6, IP66 or IP67 rating is worth preferring over IPX4 or IPX5.

## Bottom line

Robot mowers can handle rain. Your lawn just shouldn't be mowed in it. Leave the rain sensor on, use a delay of a few hours (longer on clay or slopes), unplug during thunderstorms and give the dock a roof if you can. When you're shopping, treat IPX6 as the baseline, and if your yard is wet and sloped, buy AWD.
