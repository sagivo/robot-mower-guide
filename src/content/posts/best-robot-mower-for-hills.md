## Percent grade vs degrees: the slope conversion table

Mower makers quote slopes in two different units, and mixing them up is the most common way buyers overestimate a mower. **Percent grade** is rise divided by run, times 100. **Degrees** is the angle of the ground. A 100% grade (one foot up for every foot forward) is 45°, not 100°.

[[diagram:slope]]

| Percent grade | Degrees | What it feels like | Mowers rated for it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15% | 8.5° | Gentle roll | Nearly any robot |
| 20% | 11.3° | Noticeable slope | Nearly any robot |
| 30% | 16.7° | Steep for a push mower | Navimow i105N, WORX Vision Cloud (2WD) |
| 35% | 19.3° | Hard to walk with a mower | Most 2WD at their limit |
| 45% | 24.2° | Steep lawn bank | Navimow i2 AWD, Automower iQ, many 2WD |
| 50% | 26.6° | Very steep, mow up and down | Navimow X3, GOAT A-series |
| 70% | 35.0° | Hard to stand on when wet | Sunseeker X7 Gen 2, Automower 435 iQ AWD |
| 80% | 38.7° | Scrambling territory | LUBA 3 AWD, Dreame A3 AWD / Pro, LUBA mini 2 AWD |
| 84% | 40.0° | Near the wheeled limit | Navimow X4, Landroid Vision Cloud 4WD |
| 100% | 45.0° | Extreme | Lymow One Plus (claimed) |

So when eufy lists its E-series at 18°, that's about 32%. When Lymow says 45°, that's about 100%. Convert before you compare.

## How to measure your lawn's slope

You don't need a surveyor. Find the steepest 10 feet of lawn the mower will actually cover, not the average, then use one of these:

1. **Phone method (fastest).** Lay a straight board on the steepest section and set your phone on it. On an iPhone, the Measure app's Level reads the angle; on Android, any free inclinometer app works. Take readings in two or three spots and keep the highest.
2. **Level and tape method (most accurate).** Rest one end of a 4-foot level on the uphill ground, hold it level, and measure the gap from its downhill end to the grass. Divide the gap by 48 inches and multiply by 100. A 12" gap is 25% (14°).
3. **Stake and string method (for long banks).** Drive two stakes 10 feet apart up the slope, tie a level string between them, and measure the drop at the downhill stake. A 3-foot drop over 10 feet is 30% (16.7°).

Plug the number into our [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/), which maps it to mower types and adds a safety margin. For the physics behind traction, cross-slopes and battery drain, read our full [guide to robot mower slope limits](/posts/robot-mower-slope-limits/).

## Boundary slope vs in-area slope limits

Here's the detail most "best of" lists skip. Many mowers have **two** slope limits: one for slopes inside the work area and a lower one where the slope meets the boundary. At the edge, the mower has to stop, turn and reverse on the incline, and if it slides, it slides out of the mapped area, possibly toward a road, pond or drop-off.

A few makers publish both numbers:

- **Husqvarna Automower 435 iQ AWD:** 70% in the area, 50% at the boundary.
- **Husqvarna Automower 410/420/440 iQ:** 45% in the area, only 15% at the boundary.
- **ECOVACS GOAT A-series LiDAR Pro:** 50% in the area, 20% across virtual boundaries.

Most other brands publish one figure, which you should read as the best case. Check the manual for an edge or boundary limit before buying. If your steepest slope runs right to the lawn edge, especially downhill toward a hazard, set the boundary a few feet back from the edge and keep a flat buffer strip, or choose a mower whose rating clears that slope with plenty of room.

## Wet grass and other things that shrink a slope rating

Every slope rating on this page assumes dry, short grass and a mower driving straight up and down. Real lawns are rarely all three. Plan for these:

- **Dew and rain.** Wet grass is the biggest traction killer. A mower that climbs 70% at 2 p.m. may slip on 50% at 6 a.m. Schedule steep zones for midday and enable rain delay. See [whether robot mowers work in rain](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/) for scheduling tips.
- **Cross-slopes.** Driving across a hill is harder than climbing it, because the mower slides sideways. Most apps let you set the mowing direction, so run stripes up and down the steepest bank.
- **Long or thick grass.** Spring growth adds drag and hides soft ground. Mow more often at a slightly higher cut.
- **Soft soil and clippings.** Owners of the LUBA mini 2 AWD report grass buildup underneath in wet conditions, and camera-only budget mowers like the eufy E-series slip on damp grass.
- **Battery drain.** Climbing uses more energy, so expect lower real-world coverage than the rated area on a hilly lot. Buying a size up helps (our [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) factors in slope).

Our rule of thumb: buy a mower rated at least **20% above** your steepest measured slope, in percent terms. A 50% bank calls for a mower rated 60% or more, and a 65% bank calls for 80%.

## What to look for in a robot mower for steep slopes

- **Drive type.** AWD or tracks for anything over about 35% (19°). Two-wheel drive is fine on gentle rolls. Our [guide to choosing a robot mower](/posts/how-to-choose-a-robot-lawn-mower/) covers the other trade-offs.
- **Navigation under cover.** Hilly lots often have trees. LiDAR models (Dreame A3 AWD Pro, LUBA 3 AWD, LUBA mini 2 AWD) keep their position under canopy better than RTK-plus-camera designs. Our [navigation explainer](/posts/robot-mower-navigation-explained/) goes deeper.
- **Published boundary limit.** If the maker lists one, compare it to the slope at your lawn edges.
- **Deck width and coverage headroom.** Slopes cost runtime. A wide deck (17" on the X4, 16" on the Lymow, 15.7"–15.8" on the LUBA 3 and A3 AWD Pro) claws that time back.
- **Turf impact.** Heavier AWD mowers can scuff soft turf on tight turns. Gentle U-turn settings and mowing in dry conditions help.
- **Cutting height.** Slopes look and hold better with longer grass. The Husqvarna 435's 2.8" max is a real limit; most AWD rivals reach about 4".
- **Anti-theft.** A $2,000-plus mower on a visible hillside needs 4G tracking. Read our [robot mower theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).
