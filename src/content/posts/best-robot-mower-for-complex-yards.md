## Why RTK mowers struggle under trees

Most wire-free mowers position themselves with RTK: satellite GNSS signals corrected by a base station in your yard or a network of stations over cellular ("network RTK"). In open sky, RTK is accurate to about an inch. Its weakness is that the mower itself needs a clear view of many satellites at once.

[[diagram:navigation]]

Three things break that in complex yards:

- **Canopy blocks signals.** Dense leaves absorb and scatter satellite signals. A mower under a big maple may see too few satellites to keep a precise "fixed" position.
- **Walls and fences reflect them.** Near a house or tall privacy fence, signals bounce before reaching the mower (called multipath), which makes the position jump around. Side yards are the classic trouble spot, since a two-story wall blocks half the sky.
- **Network RTK doesn't fix it.** Antenna-free network RTK removes the base station, not the need for sky view at the mower.

When RTK degrades, mowers slow down, wander off their stripes, stop with a positioning error or leave unmowed patches. Many RTK models use cameras to bridge short gaps, and that works for a passing tree, but not for a lawn that sits mostly under canopy.

**LiDAR avoids the problem.** A LiDAR mower scans its surroundings with lasers and localizes by matching what it sees (trunks, walls, fences, the house) against its map. Trees become landmarks instead of interference, and it works in shade and darkness too. That's why every pick on this page uses LiDAR. ## Setting up a robot mower for multiple zones and corridors

Most complex yards aren't one lawn. They're a front yard, a backyard, a side strip and maybe a patch behind the garage. Every pick above supports multi-zone maps. Here's how to set them up so they actually work.

[[diagram:yard-map]]

1. **Map each lawn as its own zone.** Drive or walk the boundary of each area in the app. Map tight to beds and hardscape, since the mower can't cut what's outside the line.
2. **Connect zones with channels.** A channel (also called a corridor or path) is a narrow virtual route the mower drives between zones, often across a patio, path or driveway. Draw it along the smoothest, flattest route available.
3. **Add keep-out zones.** Mark flower beds, ponds, play sets, vegetable gardens and severe root areas as no-go zones. Mapping a fixed obstacle is more reliable than trusting avoidance to see it every time.
4. **Schedule zones separately.** Shady areas grow more slowly than sunny ones. Most apps let you mow zones on different days or at different heights.
5. **Watch the first few runs.** Expect to adjust boundaries, channel routes and dock placement after the first week. That's normal for every brand.

**Where channels go wrong:** a step or curb taller than the mower can climb, gravel that spins wheels, a slope across the channel steeper than the boundary rating (the GOAT A-series is rated for just 20% across virtual boundaries), and channels that run through deep shade on an RTK-only mower. Lawns separated by a public sidewalk or street are a different matter: check local rules and your mower's settings, and plan to carry it if needed.

## Narrow passages and minimum widths

Side yards and gates are where complex-yard setups most often fail. Two things matter: physical clearance and navigation quality inside the gap.

**Physical clearance.** Brands publish different minimums, and many don't publish one for wire-free models. Figures we've seen range from about 2 feet (Husqvarna's guidance for wired installations using a guide wire) through roughly 28 to 31 inches (Roborock's published minimums for its 2WD and AWD mowers) to 4 feet (Robomow). Check your model's manual. As a rule of thumb:

- **Plan for at least 3 feet** of clear, level width for a wire-free mower.
- **Give big AWD models more room.** A 41 lb LUBA 3 with a 15.7-inch deck needs space to turn and correct its line. Compact mowers like the Sunseeker S4, Navimow i215 and LUBA mini 2 AWD fit tight gates more easily.
- **Keep gates open on mowing days**, or add a channel only on days you leave them open.

**Navigation inside the gap.** A 3-foot strip between a house wall and a fence is exactly where RTK is weakest: half the sky is blocked and the walls cause reflections. LiDAR is at its best here, because walls give it clean, close-range features to lock onto. If your yard depends on a side passage, that's a strong argument for a LiDAR mower.

## Obstacle avoidance: what to expect

Complex yards are full of obstacles: trunks, roots, garden hoses, toys, pet waste, fallen branches and furniture. Every model here combines LiDAR or 3D sensing with AI cameras, and that's the strongest avoidance setup available. It still isn't perfect.

- **Big, fixed objects** (trunks, walls, play sets) are handled reliably, especially once mapped.
- **Small, low objects** (hoses, thin sticks, toys) are the weak spot. BGR notes the GOAT A3000 occasionally gets stuck and has to be picked up and moved, and the Dreame A3 AWD's avoidance trails the flagships.
- **Roots and bumps** are a drivetrain question. AWD models (LUBA 3, A3 AWD Pro, LUBA mini 2, A3 AWD) ride over them more reliably than 2WD mowers.
- **Pets and wildlife** deserve extra caution. Schedule runs when pets are inside, and avoid night mowing if hedgehogs or other small animals visit. Our [pet, kid and wildlife safety guide](/posts/robot-mower-safety-pets-kids-wildlife/) has the details.

A five-minute sweep for hoses and toys before each run solves most problems. If your mower keeps getting stuck in the same spot, our [troubleshooting guide](/posts/robot-mower-problems-troubleshooting/) covers fixes.

## How to choose the best robot mower for complex yards

Run through these questions before you buy:

1. **How much of your lawn is under canopy or next to tall walls?** A little: an RTK-plus-vision mower may cope. A lot: buy LiDAR.
2. **How steep is it?** Over about 45% grade (24 degrees), you need AWD. Measure with our [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/).
3. **How big is the total mowing area?** Add up every zone, then buy a tier with some headroom. Coverage ratings assume ideal layouts, and complex yards mow slower. The [size matcher](/tools/size-matcher/) handles the math.
4. **How narrow is your tightest passage?** If it's under 3 feet, favor a compact mower.
5. **How much edging can you tolerate?** Complex yards have lots of edges. The GOAT A-series trims them automatically; the LUBA mini 2 and Dreame A3 AWD get closer than most.
6. **Is 4G tracking important?** For yards visible from the street, yes. See our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).
