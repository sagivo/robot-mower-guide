A robot lawn mower is worth it for most people who currently pay for weekly mowing. In our worked examples, a budget mower costs about **$1,471 over five years**, a mid-range one about **$2,365** and a premium large-lawn mower about **$3,581**, all-in. Five years of $55-a-visit lawn service costs about **$8,760**. Most buyers break even in the first or second season.

That's the short version. Below is the full math with real October 2026 models and prices: purchase price, electricity, blades, 4G data plans and a battery replacement, plus the situations where a robot mower isn't worth it. Want the answer for your own numbers? The [robot mower cost calculator](/tools/cost-calculator/) takes about 60 seconds.

## What a lawn service actually costs

Weekly mowing in most US suburbs runs roughly **$40–$80 per visit** in 2026, depending on region, lot size and whether edging and blowing are included. Over a 30-visit season (roughly April to October, longer in the South):

| Service level | Per visit | Per season (30 visits) |
|---|---|---|
| Basic mow, lower-cost market | $40 | $1,200 |
| Typical suburban mow and edge | $55 | $1,650 |
| Mow, edge and cleanup, or a large lot | $80 | $2,400 |

Those are typical ranges, not quotes. Get two local prices, because your market could be well outside them. Services also tend to raise rates over time; our 5-year math assumes 3% a year.

The service model has hidden costs too. You pay whether or not the grass needed cutting, you're on their schedule, and after a rainy week the crew takes off two weeks of growth at once, which stresses the turf.

## Running costs: electricity, blades, 4G and the battery

### Electricity and blades: about $80 a year

Robot mowers are low-power machines. Electricity for a typical suburban season runs roughly $20–$40. The small pivoting razor blades need swapping every few weeks in peak season, and a year's supply typically costs $20–$40. We round the total up to **$80 a year** to cover the odd brush or wheel cleaning kit, the same figure our [cost calculator](/tools/cost-calculator/) uses.

### 4G data plans: $0 to about $33 a year

Many 2026 mowers include a 4G connection for theft tracking, remote control and, on network-RTK models, satellite corrections. You don't need it to mow, but on network-RTK mowers the connection is part of how they navigate, and tracking is your main theft protection. Published terms vary:

Where a brand doesn't publish renewal pricing, we budget **$33 a year** (Segway's published rate) once the included period ends. That's an assumption, so confirm current terms with the manufacturer before you buy.

### Battery replacement: one in five years

The battery is the main wear part. How long it lasts depends on lawn size, slopes, heat and how you store the mower over winter. Replacement prices aren't consistently published, so our tables budget **one battery in year 4: about $150 for a budget mower, $200 mid-range and $250 premium**. WORX owners have an advantage: the Landroid runs on WORX Power Share batteries shared with the brand's tools, and WORX is stocked at Home Depot, Lowe's and Walmart, so spares should be easy to find. Our guide to [how long robot mowers last](/posts/how-long-do-robot-mowers-last/) covers battery life in more detail.

### Repairs and warranty

Robot mowers have no engine, belts, spark plugs or oil. Warranty length varies; Husqvarna's 4-year coverage on the Automower iQ line is one of the longest. Out-of-warranty failures happen, mainly to wheel motors and batteries, so check what the brand offers before you buy. Regular cleaning and proper winter storage prevent most problems; see our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/).

## Costs people forget (on both sides)

**In the robot mower's favor:**

- **Your time.** Mowing a typical suburban lawn takes about an hour or two a week. Over a season that's 30–60 hours.
- **Lawn health.** Robots trim a little often and leave fine clippings to decompose. Frequent light cutting stresses grass less than taking off a third or more once a week.
- **No scheduling.** It mows while you're at work or on vacation, and many models handle light rain (see [do robot mowers work in rain](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/)).

**In the lawn service's favor:**

- **Edges.** Most robots leave a strip along fences and beds. Budget a string trimmer and about 20 minutes every week or two. The [ECOVACS GOAT](/mowers/ecovacs-goat-a-lidar-pro/) line, with a built-in edge trimmer, is the main exception, while the [LUBA mini 2 AWD](/mowers/mammotion-luba-mini-2-awd/) and [Dreame A3 AWD](/mowers/dreame-a3-awd/) cut closer to edges than most.
- **Leaf cleanup and blowing.** Robots don't blow off the driveway or pick up fall leaves.
- **Setup and upkeep.** Expect an hour or two of setup, then blade swaps, occasional cleaning and winter storage. Our [setup guide](/posts/robot-mower-setup-guide/) shows what's involved.
- **Theft risk.** A $2,000 machine lives outside. 4G tracking, PIN locks and geofence alarms help; see [robot mower theft protection](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).
- **Upfront cash.** $1,700 today is a real difference from $55 a week.

## Robot mower vs mowing it yourself

If you mow your own lawn now, the money comparison changes. A gas push mower is cheap to buy and runs on a few hundred dollars a year in fuel, oil, blades and tune-ups at most. A riding mower costs more to buy and maintain. Our [robot mower vs riding mower](/posts/robot-mower-vs-riding-mower/) comparison goes deeper.

So the DIY decision is mostly about time and ability. At 30–60 hours a season, even modestly valuing your weekends makes a budget robot pay back quickly. If mowing is your Saturday unwind, keep doing it. If you have back problems, heat sensitivity or limited mobility, a robot mower may be worth it on quality of life alone.

## When a robot mower isn't worth it

Be honest with yourself here. The math is weaker, or doesn't work, if:

- **You only pay for occasional or every-other-week service.** At 15 visits a year, the payback period roughly doubles.
- **Your lawn is tiny.** A few hundred square feet of grass may be quicker with a cordless push mower.
- **Your yard is very steep, very rough or mostly bare.** You may need an expensive AWD or tracked model, or a robot won't cope at all. Check the [slope checker](/tools/slope-checker/) first.
- **Your lawn is wide open to the street** and you don't want to pay for a model with 4G tracking.
- **You'd still want a crew for edging and leaves.** If you keep paying a service for cleanup visits, count those in the calculator.

## When the math gets better

- **Large lawns.** Service prices climb with lot size, while a robot's running costs barely change. The premium column above saves the most against $80 service.
- **Long seasons.** In the South, where grass grows 35–40 weeks a year, service costs more and the robot pays back faster.
- **Discounts.** Dreame's A3 AWD Pro is selling well below its $3,099–$3,499 list price, and the [Navimow X3](/mowers/segway-navimow-x3/) is discounted now that the X4 is out.
