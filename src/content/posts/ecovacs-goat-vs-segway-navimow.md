In the **ECOVACS GOAT vs Segway Navimow** matchup, the GOAT is the pick for flat lawns where you want the edges trimmed for you, and the Navimow is the pick for sloped yards, lawns over three-quarters of an acre, and anyone who wants 4G theft tracking. ECOVACS builds a real string trimmer into the mower and navigates with LiDAR. Segway mostly relies on network RTK and cameras, includes 4G on every model, and offers all-wheel drive up to an 84% slope rating.

Both brands sell several sizes, so we've matched them by price band below, based on October 2026 street prices, which change often.

## The 30-second answer

- **Buy an [ECOVACS GOAT A2000 or A3000 LiDAR Pro](amazon:ecovacs-goat-a-lidar-pro)** if your lawn is flat to gently sloped, up to 0.75 acre, shaded by trees, and you're tired of string trimming. It's the most hands-off mower in the class.
- **Buy a [Segway Navimow X4](amazon:segway-navimow-x4)** if you have hills, more than 0.75 acre, or a yard visible from the street. AWD, an 84% rating and 4G tracking make it the more robust choice.
- **On a budget?** The Navimow i105N/i110N is the cheapest credible option for small, flat lawns. The GOAT O1000 costs more but trims edges.

## Navigation: LiDAR vs network RTK

Every GOAT navigates with **dual LiDAR**: a 360° spinning sensor plus a 3D time-of-flight sensor, backed by an AI camera on the A-series. It doesn't use satellites, so trees, house walls and fences don't affect positioning, and there's no antenna to place. BGR's Christian de Looper called the A3000 LiDAR Pro "easily the best setup experience I've had with a robotic mower," with auto-mapping producing a detailed 3D map in about five minutes.

Most Navimows use **network RTK**: satellite positioning corrected over a 4G connection, fused with cameras. It's very accurate under open sky and needs no base station, but it depends on satellite visibility and cellular coverage at the dock. How-To Geek's Cory Gunther moved his X430's dock three times to find a good spot and noted that "if you can't get a good connection, it won't mow." Once settled, the X430 auto-mapped his backyard perimeter in under 10 minutes. Some yards still need the X4's included RTK antenna.

The exception is the **Navimow i215 LiDAR**, which uses solid-state LiDAR and a camera like the GOAT. TechHive's Michael Ansaldo found it mapped a complex 0.18-acre yard with trees and slopes and "never wandered into areas it wasn't supposed to mow."

**Rule of thumb:** big trees or tall buildings over much of the lawn, lean GOAT or i215. Open, sunny lawn, network RTK works well and the X4's speed pays off. ## Terrain and slopes: Navimow has the range

ECOVACS builds the GOAT for flat-to-moderate lawns. The **O1000 is rated 45% (24.2°)**, and the **A2000/A3000 50% (26.6°) inside the work area but only 20% (11.3°) across virtual boundaries**, all on rear-wheel drive with front casters. That boundary limit matters if your lawn edge runs along a bank. BGR also notes the A3000 Pro occasionally gets stuck and has to be picked up and moved.

Navimow covers the whole range:

- **i105N/i110N:** 30% (16.7°), 2WD. Fine for flat lots, but it struggles on slopes and in mud.
- **i2 AWD:** 45% (24.2°) with all-wheel drive and off-road wheels that handle roots and 1.5" steps. Same slope number as the O1000, but AWD grips far better on damp grass.
- **i215 LiDAR:** 45% per Navimow EU, though one secondary source lists 30%. 2WD.
- **X4:** 84% (40.0°) with 4WD, tied with the WORX Vision Cloud 4WD for the highest rating of any wheeled mainstream mower we track.

Manufacturer ratings assume dry, short grass, so leave a margin. ## Cutting and edges: the GOAT's signature feature

**TruEdge** is a spinning string trimmer mounted on the side of every GOAT LiDAR Pro, and it's the reason to buy one. De Looper found it replaced roughly 75–90% of his manual edging, with some corner touch-ups still needed. Bob Vila's review headline put it simply: ECOVACS' mower "finally does what others won't: handle the edges." Two catches: the trimmer is loud (about 81–82 dB), and BGR noted mowing and edging have to be scheduled as separate jobs.

Navimow doesn't trim. The X4's **EdgeSense** drives the mower within about 2" of boundaries, which Gunther found effective, though it once drove onto his raised patio and damaged the epoxy coating. On the i-series, plan on regular string trimming; Ansaldo saw the i215 slip off the grass into a trench along a narrow fence line, since it relies on the map rather than cliff sensors.

On **deck width and height**, the A-series's 13" deck is wide for its price, and the X4's 17" deck is the widest in the class. Cool-season lawns kept at 3.5"+ fit the X4 (up to 4.0"), the i105N and i2 AWD (3.6") and the GOAT A-series (3.54"), but not the O1000 (3.15") or i215 (2.8"). De Looper also found the GOAT sometimes mistakes tall grass for obstacles and leaves uncut patches.

## App, 4G and anti-theft: Navimow's clear win

This is the biggest practical gap between the brands.

- **ECOVACS:** the O1000 connects over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth only, and ECOVACS lists no 4G or theft tracking. The A2000/A3000 use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi with an optional cellular module, and ECOVACS doesn't list anti-theft features. Check the current spec sheet before buying, and plan on strong Wi-Fi across the lawn.
- **Navimow:** every current model includes 4G and GPS tracking, with geofence alarms on the i2 AWD, i215 and X4. The i2 AWD and i215 include one year of 4G; after that, Navimow Connect+ costs about $33 a year ($32.90, per TechHive).

A mid-range mower with no tracking is a real risk if your front lawn faces the street. Our [robot mower theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/) covers PIN locks, alarms, AirTags and garage options.

On the apps themselves, de Looper found ECOVACS Home intuitive, and Gunther highlighted Navimow's scheduling, weather settings, night mowing, child lock and Alexa/Google voice control.

## Support and warranty

Both are large consumer-robotics brands selling direct and through Amazon, with support handled remotely and parts shipped rather than through local dealers. Navimow's US warranty policy lists 3 years on the X430, with a separate 2-year term on the battery. Confirm the term for i-series models. ECOVACS's European retailers list 3 years for the A3000 LiDAR Pro, but confirm the US term on the listing before you buy.

Either way, routine care matters more than the warranty length. The GOAT's trimmer line is a consumable, and every robot needs blade swaps and cleaning. See our [maintenance guide](/posts/robot-mower-maintenance/) and [troubleshooting guide](/posts/robot-mower-problems-troubleshooting/).

## Which should you buy?

### Buy an ECOVACS GOAT if...

- Your lawn is flat to gently sloped (under about 25°)
- Edging is the chore you most want gone
- Trees or buildings shade much of the yard
- Your lawn is 0.75 acre or less and not visible from the street, or you'll add the cellular module

**Skip it if** you have real hills, need theft tracking out of the box, or your yard is full of sticks and hoses.

## Final verdict

For **flat, shaded lawns up to 0.75 acre**, the **ECOVACS GOAT A2000/A3000 LiDAR Pro** is the more hands-off mower: LiDAR that ignores trees, a wide 13" deck, and the only built-in edge trimmer in the mainstream market. Just factor in the missing 4G tracking.

For **everything else** (slopes, bigger lots, street-facing yards), **Segway Navimow** is the better ecosystem. The X4 is our highest-scored mower, the i2 AWD brings AWD to small lots at a budget-tier price, and every model ships with 4G tracking.
