Robot mower maintenance is light but not optional: brush off clippings and wipe the sensors weekly, change the blades every 1–3 months of mowing, keep firmware current, and store the mower and dock indoors over a freezing winter. That's about 10–15 minutes a week in season and an hour or two each spring and fall. Skip it and you get a ragged cut, docking failures and, worst of all, a battery that doesn't wake up in April.

This guide is compiled from manufacturer care instructions (Husqvarna and Mammotion publish detailed ones), published reviews and owner reports. Always check your own manual first, since brands differ on details like storage charge.

## Seasonal robot mower maintenance calendar

Dates assume a typical northern US season. In the South and on the West Coast, where some lawns are mowed year-round, do the spring and fall service anyway and run a lighter winter schedule.

| When | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **Early spring** (Mar–Apr) | Bring the dock back out and reconnect it. Fully charge the mower. Update the app and firmware. Fit new blades if you didn't in the fall. Walk the lawn for winter debris. Check the map, no-go zones and channels. Start with a higher cut. |
| **Late spring** (May–Jun) | Peak growth. Weekly underside clean. Check blades every couple of weeks. Lower the cut height gradually. Clear grass from the wheels. |
| **Summer** (Jul–Aug) | Raise the height a notch in heat and drought. Wipe the LiDAR window and cameras weekly (dust builds up). Check the charging contacts. Second or third blade change. |
| **Fall** (Sep–Oct) | Clear leaves often, since they hide obstacles and clog the deck. Reduce the schedule as growth slows. Look for tire and wheel wear. |
| **Late fall** (Nov) | Final mow. Deep clean. Fit new blades and screws. Charge to storage level, switch off and store indoors. Bring the dock in. |
| **Winter** (Dec–Feb) | Check the battery once or twice and top it up if your manual says to. Keep the mower dry and above freezing. |

## How often to change robot mower blades

Most wire-free robots use small, pivoting razor-style blades on a spinning disc. They're designed to be cheap and disposable, not sharpened. The usual interval is **every 1–3 months of mowing**, and wear depends on your yard:

- **Faster wear:** sandy soil, lots of twigs, pine cones, acorns or gravel edges, and large lawns that run many hours a week.
- **Slower wear:** small, clean, well-kept lawns.

Change them early if you notice:

- Grass tips that look torn, frayed or brown a day after mowing
- A rougher or louder sound from the deck
- Reduced coverage or more frequent recharging, since dull blades take more power

**How to swap them safely:**

1. Switch the mower off and enable any child or PIN lock. Wear cut-resistant gloves.
2. Turn the mower over on a soft surface like a towel or grass. Don't rest it on the LiDAR dome or cameras.
3. Remove each blade screw with the included tool and fit new blades with new screws.
4. Replace the **whole set at once** so the disc stays balanced.
5. Spin the disc by hand to confirm each blade pivots freely.

Husqvarna recommends fitting new blades and screws before winter storage, which also prevents rust over the off-season. If you own an ECOVACS GOAT with TruEdge, check the edge trimmer's line or head per the manual as well.

## Cleaning: what's safe, and what IP ratings mean

Grass packed under the deck is the most common cause of a poor cut, and it's worse in wet weather. Owners of the LUBA mini 2 AWD, for example, report buildup underneath on damp lawns. Clean weekly in peak season:

- **Underside and blade disc:** brush or scrape off caked clippings with a stiff brush or plastic scraper.
- **Wheels:** clear packed grass and mud from treads and wheel wells. Clogged treads cost traction on slopes.
- **Sensors:** wipe the LiDAR window, cameras and any ultrasonic sensors with a soft microfiber cloth. A smudged lens causes navigation errors.
- **Charging contacts:** wipe the contacts on the mower and the dock with a dry cloth, and check for corrosion. Dirty contacts are a top cause of "won't charge" complaints ([more fixes here](/posts/robot-mower-problems-troubleshooting/)).

### What IP ratings mean for washing

An IP rating has two digits. The first is dust protection (6 means dust-tight, X means not tested). The second is water protection.

| Rating | Water protection | Safe cleaning | 2026 models we track |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPX4 | Splashing from any direction | Brush and damp cloth only | Automower 435 iQ AWD |
| IPX5 | Water jets from a 6.3 mm nozzle | Brush; a light hose rinse if the manual allows | Sunseeker X7 Gen 2, Sunseeker X5, Automower iQ |
| IPX6 / IP66 | Powerful jets from a 12.5 mm nozzle | Brush plus a garden hose | LUBA 3 AWD, Navimow X4, Dreame A3 AWD and A3 AWD Pro, GOAT A and O1000, Sunseeker S4, eufy E15/E18, Lymow, Roborock RockMow X1 and X1 LiDAR, HOOKII Neomow X2, Airseekers TRON, ANTHBOT M5 LiDAR, Greenworks AiMowbot C30Z (IPX6); Navimow i2 AWD, i105N, X3 (IP66) |
| IP67 | Temporary immersion up to 1 m | Brush plus a garden hose | Mammotion YUKA mini 2 |
| Not published | Unknown | Treat as IPX4 | WORX Landroid Vision Cloud models, US Navimow i215, Anthbot M9, MOVA LiDAX Ultra AWD, Yarbo Mower Pro |

**Pressure washers:** don't use one on a mower rated below IPX6. Even on IPX6, IP66 and IP67 models, a pressure washer is far more forceful than the rating test, and Mammotion's own care guidance says to avoid them. If your manual specifically allows it, use the lowest setting from a distance and keep it away from the LiDAR, cameras, charging contacts and seams. A brush and a garden hose do the job without the risk.

Weather ratings also matter for rain. Our [rain guide](/posts/do-robot-mowers-work-in-rain/) covers mowing in wet weather.

## Robot mower battery care

The battery is the main wear item. Owners and reviewers generally expect it to last a few seasons before capacity noticeably drops, while the chassis and motors last much longer. Our [lifespan guide](/posts/how-long-do-robot-mowers-last/) has the full picture. To get the most from it:

- **Let the mower manage charging.** Docking after each session is better for the battery than running it flat.
- **Avoid deep discharge.** A battery left at 0% for weeks may not recover. This is how most winter failures happen.
- **Mind the temperature.** Heat speeds up aging, and freezing can cause permanent damage. Don't leave a mower that's off in a hot shed or a freezing garage.
- **Use genuine replacements.** When capacity fades, buy the manufacturer's pack. Prices vary widely by brand, so check the parts page before you need one. WORX owners can share batteries across the Power Share tool range.

The Lymow One Plus uses a LiFePO4 battery, a chemistry generally known for long cycle life, but the same storage rules still apply.

## Robot mower winter storage

If your winters bring freezing temperatures, here's the end-of-season routine:

1. **Deep clean** the deck, wheels, sensors and contacts. Let everything dry.
2. **Fit new blades and screws** (Husqvarna's advice) so they don't rust in place.
3. **Charge to storage level.** Generic lithium-ion advice is about 40–60%, but brands differ. Mammotion recommends around 80%, and Husqvarna says to fully charge Automowers before switching them off. The aim is the same: don't let the battery drift to zero over a four-month winter. Follow your manual.
4. **Switch it off fully** and store it indoors in a dry place above 32°F. Mammotion suggests about 50–68°F as ideal.
5. **Check it mid-winter.** Top up the charge if your manual calls for it.

**The dock:** unplug it, clean the contacts and bring it indoors. Both Husqvarna and Mammotion advise against leaving the charging station out. Coil and protect the power cable, and cover the outdoor outlet.

**The RTK antenna:** Husqvarna says its EPOS reference station is weatherproof and can stay out (rated for about -4°F to 158°F). For other brands, check the manual. If you take an antenna down, mark its exact position, since moving it can mean recalibrating or remapping in spring. Network-RTK and LiDAR mowers have nothing to take down besides the dock.

Theft risk doesn't disappear in the off-season, either. Store the mower somewhere locked, and see our [theft protection guide](/posts/robot-mower-theft-protection/).

## Firmware and app updates

Robot mowers improve over time through software. Navigation, obstacle avoidance and scheduling often get better through updates.

- **Update before the season** and whenever the app prompts, ideally with the mower docked and charged.
- **Don't interrupt an update.** Keep the mower on the dock with a strong Wi-Fi or 4G signal until it finishes.
- **Read the release notes.** Updates sometimes change behavior, like edge passes or obstacle sensitivity, so check your map and zones afterward.
- **Keep connectivity alive.** If your included 4G period ends, decide whether to renew. Without it, you may lose remote tracking, and updates will rely on Wi-Fi reaching the mower.
